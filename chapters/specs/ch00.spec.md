### Beat 1 — What the Extra license opens
- The upgrade in one sentence: "all available U.S. Amateur Radio operating privileges on all bands and all modes" (ARRL's own line, pinned in canon §2.6). Extra is the top of the US license structure — the FCC currently issues only three classes, and there is no element beyond Element 4.
- The honest map: the Extra-only HF slices, exactly (canon §2.1 — 3.500–3.525, 3.600–3.800, 7.000–7.025, 7.125–7.175 (R2), 14.000–14.025, 14.150–14.225, 21.000–21.025, 21.200–21.275 MHz) — Extra-exclusive spectrum exists only on 80, 75, 40, 20, and 15 m. The legacy-Advanced nuance (they share parts of those slices) gets one sentence. Why it's worth it: the DX-heavy band bottoms (a rare-DX CW station at 14.005 MHz can only be answered by US Extras), thinner pileups, prime real estate — r5 §7's color.
- Beyond spectrum: CEPT full privileges (Extra/Advanced → CEPT T/R 61-01, General only → CEPT Novice — a real travel benefit, DA 16-1048; the three-document carry); Group A vanity call-sign formats (1×2, 2×1, 2×2 A-first) reserved to Extra; VE eligibility for all three exam levels including administering Element 4 itself. 60 m is NOT an Extra prize (shared by General and above — r5 §7).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch00-what-extra-opens}}`.
- Vocabulary: license class, privileges, CEPT, incentive licensing (historical aside), vanity call sign.

### Beat 2 — The capstone exam (Element 4)
- One exam, not three: a General upgrading passes **Element 4 only** — the General license credits Elements 2 and 3 (§97.505(a)(2)).
- The exam's shape: **50 questions, one drawn from each of the pool's 50 groups, out of the public 599-question 2024–2028 Extra pool** (10 subelements E1–E9 and E0; E7 and E9 reach group letter H); **37 correct to pass** — print "37 of 50," never "74%" as the authoritative figure (canon §7.4: the rule pins a count, not a percentage; "74%" is derived arithmetic and flagged as such if used at all).
- The pool is public and fixed for exams 2024-07-01 → **2028-06-30** — no surprise questions; every exam question is verbatim in Appendix A of this book. Say the expiry plainly and early. Four errata incorporated (2024-01-31, 2024-11-08, 2025-09-24/25, 2026-02-04); the four withdrawn questions (E9E10, E2A13, E6D07, E4D05) simply don't exist — never look for them, never quote them.
- The stakes: 50 questions across 50 groups means every group is guaranteed exactly one seat on every exam — no topic may be skipped. E6–E0 supply 30 of the 50 seats (E7+E9 alone 16).
- Vocabulary: Element 4, question pool, subelement, group, NCVEC, errata, CSCE.

### Beat 3 — Exam day, for upgraders
- You already have an FRN from your General grant — no new CORES step. (If a reader somehow lacks one: registering in CORES "carries no fee and no exam requirement" — never print "free of charge," canon §7.10.)
- Find a session: ARRL's session finder and hamstudy.org/sessions; exams are run by teams of at least 3 VEs under a VEC, and an Element 4 session's VEs must each hold Extra. **Wording law (canon §7.10):** remote video-supervised exams exist but availability depends entirely on the individual VE team (Laurel runs in-person only) — never promise remote testing.
- Fees: the ARRL VEC session fee is $15.00 ($5.00 under 18 — calendar-2026 figures; Laurel VEC charges nothing). **The upgrade exemption:** the FCC's $35 application fee does not apply to operator-class upgrades — no payment step, no 10-day payment window (contrast with Book 2's new-license flow).
- What to bring: one legal photo ID (or two non-photo IDs), your FRN, the session fee; the NCVEC Quick-Form 605 (2022 edition) is completed at the session — email and FRN mandatory; every applicant answers the Basic Qualification Question.
- Vocabulary: FRN, CORES, VEC, VE, exam session, Form 605, CSCE.

### Beat 4 — After you pass: operate immediately, signing /AE
- The upgrade-immediacy rule (§97.9(b)): once the Form 605 requesting the higher class is properly submitted to the administering VEs **and** you hold the CSCE showing the pass, you may exercise Extra privileges immediately — before the VEC files anything and before ULS changes — until final disposition of the application or 365 days, whichever comes first.
- **Wording law (canon §7.3/§7.10, binding):** never write "transmit as soon as you pass" without both conditions (Form 605 properly submitted + CSCE in hand) **and** the /AE identification requirement in the same breath.
- The /AE indicator (§97.119(f)(3) — (f)(2) is AG for General upgrades, do not confuse them; §97.119(c)): while using the new privileges, append AE to your call sign — say "temporary AE" on phone ("This is KX9ABC temporary AE"), sign KX9ABC/AE on CW or digital; drop it once ULS shows Amateur Extra. (The "temporary AE"/"Interim"/"Alpha Echo" phrasing is one VEC's practice, not rule text — the rule requires only the AE indicator separated by "/" or a suitable word.)
- Contrast with Book 2's new licensees: a first-time licensee has **no** authority until the grant appears in ULS — immediacy is for existing licensees only. You keep your call sign when you upgrade. Grant timing: "your ULS record typically updates within days" — never print a day count as a promise (canon §7.10).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch00-upgrade-journey-ae}}`.
- Vocabulary: CSCE, ULS, /AE indicator, upgrade authority, final disposition.
