### Beat 1 — Gain, ERP/EIRP, efficiency (E9A)
- The reference: the isotropic radiator is a hypothetical, lossless antenna radiating equally in all directions (E9A01); a half-wave dipole gains 2.15 dB over isotropic, so dBd = dBi − 2.15 (E9A12: 6 dBi = 3.85 dBd).
- The pairing law: **ERP pairs with dBd, EIRP with dBi** — ERP is the total radiated-power figure accounting for all gains and losses (E9A03). The arithmetic chain (worked in §5 with all three pool examples): ERP = TPO × 10^((gains − losses)/10) — sign discipline first (subtract losses, add gains): E9A02 → 286 W; E9A06 → 317 W; E9A07 (asks EIRP, gives dBi) → 252 W. The trap anatomy: adding losses instead of subtracting (469 W), treating dB as a linear multiplier (2000 W).
- Feed-point and ground: antenna **height** affects feed-point impedance — line length, tuner settings, and power level do not (E9A04); ground gain = increased signal strength from ground reflections near the antenna (E9A05); the higher the frequency the smaller the first Fresnel zone (E9A08 — 5.8 GHz is smallest of the listed).
- Efficiency: radiation resistance ÷ **total** resistance (E9A09); a ground radial system improves a ground-mounted λ/4 vertical (E9A10); soil conductivity determines HF vertical ground losses (E9A11).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch09-eirp-erp-chain}}`.
- Vocabulary: isotropic radiator, ERP, EIRP, dBd, dBi, ground gain, radiation resistance, antenna efficiency, first Fresnel zone.

### Beat 2 — Patterns and modeling: Figures E9-1 and E9-2 (E9B)
- Directivity redirects power, it does not create it — a lossless gain antenna and an isotropic radiator driven by the same power radiate the same total power (E9B07); the far field is where the pattern shape no longer varies with distance (E9B08).
- **Reading the redrawn Figure E9-1** (embed `{{fig:ch09-pool-fig-e91}}`): the free-space azimuth polar plot, rings −3/−6/−12/−24 dB. Beamwidth is measured between the −3 dB points: crossings at ±25° → **50° beamwidth** (E9B01 — 75°/30°/25° come from reading null-to-null or one-sided widths); the 180° response ≈ −18 dB → **front-to-back 18 dB** (E9B02); the 90° response ≈ −14 dB → **front-to-side 14 dB** (E9B03 — −12 dB is the sidelobe level, not the 90° level).
- **Reading the redrawn Figure E9-2** (embed `{{fig:ch09-pool-fig-e92}}`): the "Over Real Ground" elevation semicircle, arcs −10/−20/−30/−40 dB. The semicircle-above-ground format tells you it's an **elevation** pattern (E9B05); the main lobe hugs the horizon, peaking at **7.5° elevation** (E9B06 — 45/75/25 read the wrong lobes); the rear lobe cluster runs ≈ −28 dB → **front-to-back 28 dB** (E9B04).
- Modeling: the Method of Moments models a wire as a series of segments each carrying a uniform current (E9B09); dropping below ~10 segments per half-wavelength can make the computed feed-point impedance wrong (E9B10, E9B11).
- Vocabulary: beamwidth (−3 dB points), front-to-back ratio, front-to-side ratio, elevation/azimuth pattern, far field, Method of Moments, wire segment.

### Beat 3 — Wire antennas and phased arrays (E9C)
- The phased-array table (tested as a set): two λ/4 verticals spaced λ/2, fed 180° out of phase → figure-eight **along** the array axis, end-fire (E9C01); spaced λ/4, fed 90° out of phase → **cardioid** (E9C02); spaced λ/2, fed **in phase** → figure-eight **broadside** to the array axis (E9C03). In-phase broadside, out-of-phase end-fire — the phase and the orientation move together.
- Wire behavior: lengthening an unterminated long wire forms additional lobes that align increasingly with the wire axis (E9C04); a terminating resistor makes a rhombic/long-wire pattern unidirectional (E9C06).
- The name-that-antenna drill: an OCFD is fed off-center to create a similar feed-point impedance on multiple bands (E9C05); a folded dipole = λ/2 dipole with an additional parallel wire connecting its ends, ≈300 Ω feed impedance (E9C07, E9C08); a G5RV = center-fed wire through a specific length of open-wire line to a balun and coax (E9C09); a Zepp = end-fed half-wavelength (E9C10); an extended double Zepp = center-fed 1.25λ (E9C12) — the three raid each other's answer text verbatim.
- Terrain: mounting over seawater increases low-angle radiation vs soil (E9C11); raising a horizontally polarized antenna **lowers** the lowest-lobe takeoff angle (E9C13); a long downhill slope lowers the main-lobe takeoff angle in the downhill direction (E9C14).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch09-phased-array}}`.
- Vocabulary: phased array, end-fire, broadside, cardioid, OCFD, folded dipole, G5RV, Zepp, extended double Zepp, terminating resistor, takeoff angle.

### Beat 4 — Yagis, dishes, and loading (E9D)
- Doubling the operating frequency increases an ideal parabolic reflector's gain by **6 dB** — the dish is twice as many wavelengths across (E9D01). Circular polarization from linear Yagis = two Yagis on the **same axis**, perpendicular to each other, driven elements at the same point on the boom, fed 90° out of phase (E9D02 — the parallel-plane and 180° distractors).
- Loading: the most efficient loading-coil location on a short whip is near the **center** of the radiator (E9D03); loading coils should have a high reactance-to-resistance ratio (E9D04); a loading coil cancels the short antenna's capacitive reactance to resonate it (E9D09) — but Q rises and SWR bandwidth **decreases** (E9D06, E9D08 — the "increases" options are the trap); top loading improves radiation efficiency (E9D07); below resonance a base-fed whip's radiation resistance decreases (E9D10).
- Yagi anatomy: the driven element is approximately a half wavelength (E9D05 — the 234/f and 1005/f distractors are wire-length formulas in feet, not the element's electrical fraction); two-element Yagis favor a reflector over a director for higher gain (E9D11); parasitic elements are made longer or shorter than resonance to control phase shift (E9D12).
- Vocabulary: parabolic reflector, circular polarization, loading coil, top loading, radiation efficiency, radiation resistance, reflector, director, driven element, parasitic element.

### Beat 5 — Matching systems (E9E)
- The three-way description drill: a **gamma** match connects the coax shield to the element center and the center conductor — through a series capacitor that cancels the gamma section's inductive reactance — to a point a fraction of a wavelength off-center (E9E02, E9E04); it's also the match used to shunt-feed a grounded tower at its base (E9E09). A **beta (hairpin)** match needs a driven element electrically **shorter** than λ/2 (capacitive feed-point impedance) and requires the element insulated from the boom (E9E01, E9E05). A **stub** match is a short length of transmission line in parallel with the feed line at or near the feed point (E9E03).
- The Q-section (quarter-wave transformer): Z₀ = √(Z_line × Z_load) — the **geometric** mean, not arithmetic: √(50 × 100) = 70.7 Ω, so 75 Ω line is the suitable choice (E9E06 — the 62 Ω distractor is the arithmetic-mean bait; worked in §5).
- The rest of the cast: the reflection coefficient describes the interaction of a load and a transmission line (E9E07); a Wilkinson divider splits power equally between two 50 Ω loads while maintaining a 50 Ω input (E9E08); multiple driven elements connected through phasing lines control the radiation pattern (E9E11).
- One numbering note: E9E10 was withdrawn by errata 1 (2024-01-31) and the group was not renumbered — the gap is intentional (canon §1.3).
- Vocabulary: gamma match, beta/hairpin match, stub match, Q-section, shunt feed, Wilkinson divider, reflection coefficient, phasing lines.

### Beat 6 — Transmission lines (E9F)
- Velocity factor: the wave velocity in the line ÷ the velocity of light in a vacuum (E9F01 — VF **divides** the speed of light; the distractor multiplies); the dielectric has the biggest effect on it (E9F02); electrical length exceeds physical length because waves move slower in the line (E9F03).
- Line-length math (worked in §5): L(λ/2, m) ≈ 150 × VF / f(MHz) — a λ/2 air-insulated parallel line at 14.10 MHz is ≈ **10.6 m** (E9F06; air VF ≈ 1.0; the 7.0/8.5/13.3 m distractors are wrong-VF or λ/3 slips).
- Line types: open-wire parallel line has lower loss than plastic-dielectric coax (E9F07); foam vs solid dielectric coax (all else equal) = lower safe operating voltage AND lower loss per length AND higher VF (E9F08 — all three); microstrip = precision PCB conductors above a ground plane for constant-impedance microwave interconnects (E9F05).
- **The transformation table — the group's core, six questions ride it:** a λ/2 line **repeats** its termination (shorted far end → very low impedance at the input, E9F04); a λ/4 line **inverts** its termination (shorted → very high, E9F09; open → very low, E9F12); a λ/8 line converts to reactance (shorted → **inductive**, E9F10; open → **capacitive**, E9F11). Teach "short→L" via the stub-tuner intuition.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch09-stub-matching}}`.
- Vocabulary: velocity factor, electrical vs physical length, characteristic impedance, microstrip, dielectric, open-wire/ladder line, foam dielectric.

### Beat 7 — The Smith chart in full (E9G)
- What it is: a plot of normalized impedance on a coordinate system of resistance **circles** and reactance **arcs** (E9G02, E9G04, E9G10 — the distractors invert the two families); used to calculate impedance along transmission lines and impedance/SWR values (E9G01, E9G03); its classic use is determining the length and position of an impedance-matching stub (E9G05).
- Normalizing = reassigning the prime center's impedance value to the system impedance — 1.0 = 50 Ω (E9G08); a third family, constant-SWR circles centered on the prime center, is added during matching-network design (E9G09); the rim's wavelength scales are calibrated in fractions of transmission-line electrical wavelength (E9G11).
- **Reading the redrawn Figure E9-3** (embed `{{fig:ch09-pool-fig-e93}}`): the simplified normalized chart, ∞ at the RIGHT, 0 at the left, resistance labels 0.2–20 ascending along the single straight horizontal axis, reactance arcs terminating on the outer circle with matching +/− magnitudes above (inductive) and below (capacitive), prime center at 1.0. The two tested names: the large outer circle on which the reactance arcs terminate = **reactance axis** (E9G06); the only straight line = **resistance axis** (E9G07). The questions share an option pool — "reactance axis" is correct in one and a distractor in the other; the naming pair is the whole lesson.
- The stub-matching procedure in words (what E9G05 means in practice): normalize the load and plot it; draw the constant-SWR circle through it; travel along the line (rim scale, toward the generator) until the circle crosses the 1.0 resistance circle; the distance traveled sets the stub position and the reactance read there sets the stub length.
- Vocabulary: Smith chart, resistance circle, reactance arc, prime center, normalized impedance, constant-SWR circle, reactance axis, resistance axis, wavelength scale.

### Beat 8 — Receiving and direction-finding antennas (E9H)
- The Beverage: at least one wavelength long (E9H01); its terminating resistor absorbs signals arriving from the reverse direction (E9H07); the correct termination value shows as **minimum SWR variation** over the desired frequency range (E9H06 — not maximum DC current, not minimum front-to-back).
- The receiving reality: on 160/80 m atmospheric noise is so high that **directivity** matters far more than losses (E9H02); RDF — receiving directivity factor — is peak antenna gain compared to average gain over the hemisphere around and above the antenna (E9H03 — NOT front-to-back ratio, not dBi/dBd).
- DF craft: an electrostatic shield around a small DF loop eliminates unbalanced capacitive coupling, improving null depth (E9H04); a small wire loop's challenge is its bidirectional null — 180° ambiguity (E9H05); a sense antenna modifies the pattern to a null in only one direction (E9H08); a terminated single-turn loop such as a pennant is cardioid (E9H09); more turns and/or more enclosed area increases a multiturn loop's output (E9H10); the cardioid's DF value is its **single null** (E9H11 — not a sharp peak).
- Vocabulary: Beverage antenna, receiving directivity factor, sense antenna, pennant antenna, electrostatic shield, cardioid pattern, direction finding, bidirectional null.

## 5. Worked examples (several `> **Worked example:**` blockquotes — pool numbers exactly)

- The ERP/EIRP chain (mandatory): "150 W into a feed line costing 2 dB and a duplexer costing 2.2 dB, then an antenna with 7 dBd gain: the net is −2 − 2.2 + 7 = +2.8 dB, so ERP = 150 × 10^0.28 = 150 × 1.905 ≈ **286 W** (E9A02). Bigger rig: 200 W, losses −4 −3.2 −0.8 dB, +10 dBd → net +2 dB → 200 × 1.585 ≈ **317 W** (E9A06). Asked as EIRP with the gain given in dBi: 200 W, −2 −2.8 −1.2 dB, +7 dBi → net +1 dB → 200 × 1.259 ≈ **252 W** (E9A07). Sign first: the 469 W distractor is what you get for adding the losses."
- dBi ↔ dBd: "A dipole gains 2.15 dB over isotropic, so a 6 dBi antenna is 6 − 2.15 = **3.85 dBd** (E9A12) — dBi is always the bigger number."
- The Q-section: "To match a 100 Ω load to a 50 Ω line with a quarter-wave section: Z₀ = √(Z_line × Z_load) = √(50 × 100) = 70.7 Ω — and the closest standard line is **75 Ω** (E9E06). The geometric mean, not the arithmetic one (that's the 62 Ω bait)."
- Line length: "A half-wavelength of air-insulated parallel line at 14.10 MHz: λ = 300/14.10 = 21.28 m, so λ/2 ≈ **10.6 m** with air's VF ≈ 1.0 (E9F06). The general form: L(λ/2, m) ≈ 150 × VF / f(MHz) — VF always divides, never multiplies (E9F01)."
- The transformation table in action: "Short the far end of a λ/2 line and the input repeats it: very low impedance (E9F04). The same short on a λ/4 line inverts: very high (E9F09). Open a λ/4 line and it inverts to very low (E9F12). At λ/8 the line becomes a reactance: shorted → inductive (E9F10), open → capacitive (E9F11)."
- A Smith-chart stub walk (prose procedure, per E9G05): "Normalize the load (divide by 50 Ω) and plot it on the redrawn Figure E9-3; swing the constant-SWR circle through it with the compass on the prime center; walk the rim toward the generator until the circle crosses the 1.0 resistance circle; the rim distance you walked sets where the stub goes, and the reactance you read there is what the stub must cancel."
