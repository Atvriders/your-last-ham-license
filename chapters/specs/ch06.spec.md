### Beat 1 — The semiconductor junction and the transistor family (E6A)
- Doping: donor impurities add free electrons (N-type), acceptor impurities add holes (P-type) (E6A02, E6A04 — the pair is swapped in the distractors); reverse bias pulls carriers away from the junction and widens the depletion region, so no current flows (E6A03). Figure: `{{fig:ch06-depletion-region}}`.
- Materials: gallium arsenide's higher electron mobility suits microwave circuits (E6A01, E6E01).
- FET vs BJT: the FET's gate is insulated (MOSFET) or reverse-biased (JFET), so its DC input impedance is far higher than a BJT base's (E6A05); a depletion-mode FET conducts source-to-drain with zero gate voltage — an enhancement-mode device does not (E6A09).
- The numbers: beta = ΔIc/ΔIb (E6A06); a forward-biased silicon base-emitter junction drops ≈0.6–0.7 V (E6A07 — the 6–7 V and 0.6–0.7/6–7 ohm distractors swap units and magnitude); alpha cutoff frequency = where the grounded-base current gain falls to 0.7 of its 1 kHz value (E6A08 — "beta cutoff" is invented); MOSFET gate-protection Zeners protect against **static damage** (E6A12).
- **Reading Figure E6-1** (embed `{{fig:ch06-pool-fig-e61}}`): six FET symbols, and the three independent discriminators that resolve every option — (1) JFET vs MOSFET: the JFET's arrow sits ON the gate lead (symbols 1, 6); the MOSFET's gate is insulated and its arrow is internal on the source/substrate (2–5); (2) arrow-in = N-channel, arrow-out = P-channel; (3) dual-gate = G1/G2 labels (4, 5). E6A10 → symbol **4** (N-channel dual-gate MOSFET); E6A11 → symbol **1** (P-channel JFET).
- Vocabulary: donor/acceptor impurity, depletion region, N-channel/P-channel, depletion-mode/enhancement-mode FET, dual-gate MOSFET, beta, alpha cutoff frequency.

### Beat 2 — The diode family (E6B)
- The cast: a Zener holds a constant voltage drop across varying current — the reference/regulator use (E6B01); a Schottky is a metal-semiconductor junction — low forward drop, fast recovery, good as a VHF/UHF mixer/detector and as an efficient power-supply rectifier (E6B02, E6B06, E6B08 — the rectifier advantage is the lower forward drop, NOT higher reverse breakdown); an LED's forward voltage is set by the band gap (E6B03); a varactor is a voltage-controlled capacitor (E6B04 — the phrase is the tell); a PIN diode's RF resistance is set by its forward DC bias current, and its low junction capacitance makes it a good RF switch (E6B05, E6B11); a point-contact diode is an RF detector, not a high-voltage rectifier (E6B09); junction diodes fail from excessive junction temperature — current kills via heat (E6B07).
- **Reading Figure E6-2** (embed `{{fig:ch06-pool-fig-e62}}`): eight diode symbols; the Schottky's tell is the pair of squared S-hooks at both ends of the cathode bar — symbol **6** (E6B10). The distractors all have "something extra on the cathode bar": 1 (varactor-style bow), 2 (back-to-back pair), 3 (Zener wings).
- Vocabulary: Schottky barrier diode, metal-semiconductor junction, varactor, PIN diode, point-contact diode, band gap, junction capacitance.

### Beat 3 — Digital ICs and logic families (E6C)
- Comparators: hysteresis stops input noise from chattering the output (E6C01); the comparator flips state when the input crosses the threshold voltage (E6C02).
- Logic states and families: tri-state = 0, 1, and high-impedance output states for bus sharing (E6C03 — not ternary math); CMOS has the lowest power consumption of the listed families (E6C05) and its input switches at about **half** the supply voltage (E6C06 — the "twice the supply" distractor); BiCMOS = CMOS high input impedance + bipolar low output impedance (E6C04); a pull-up/pull-down resistor establishes a defined voltage on an otherwise open input or output (E6C07); FPGAs are configured in a hardware description language (E6C09).
- **Reading Figure E6-3** (embed `{{fig:ch06-pool-fig-e63}}`): six gate symbols and the 2×2 matrix — D-shape body = AND family, curved-input body = OR family; output bubble = negation; triangle = buffer (no bubble) or inverter (bubble). E6C08 (NAND) → **2**; E6C10 (NOR) → **4**; E6C11 (NOT) → **5**. E6C11's options deliberately mix bubbles from both families (2, 4) with the triangle pair (5, 6).
- Vocabulary: hysteresis, comparator, threshold voltage, tri-state logic, BiCMOS, CMOS, pull-up/pull-down resistor, FPGA, hardware description language.

### Beat 4 — Magnetics and crystals (E6D)
- Piezoelectricity is two-way: generate a voltage when stressed AND flex when a voltage is applied (E6D01, E6D03); a quartz crystal models as a series RLC motional arm in parallel with a shunt capacitance (E6D02).
- Cores: laminations reduce eddy-current loss (E6D04); permeability determines inductance (E6D06); ferrite needs fewer turns for a given inductance than powdered iron, but powdered iron has the better temperature stability (E6D05 vs E6D08 — the pool tests both directions and the distractors trade the two materials' strengths); ferrite beads are VHF/UHF parasitic suppressors at transistor amplifier terminals (E6D09).
- Shapes and slugs: a toroid confines most of its magnetic field inside the core — less stray coupling, less pickup (E6D10); inserting brass into a coil **decreases** inductance (E6D11 — aluminum appears as a distractor in both directions); saturation = operation at excessive magnetic flux (E6D12).
- One numbering note: E6D07 was withdrawn by the 3rd errata and the group was not renumbered — the gap is intentional (canon §1.3).
- Vocabulary: piezoelectricity, motional arm, permeability, eddy currents, lamination, ferrite bead, saturation, toroid, slug tuning.

### Beat 5 — MMICs, packages, and SMT (E6E)
- Materials at the top end: GaAs's electron mobility suits UHF and above (E6E01); GaN supports the highest MMIC operating frequency of the listed materials (E6E03).
- The MMIC block: 50-ohm-in/50-ohm-out with controlled gain, low noise figure, and constant input/output impedance over its specified range (E6E04, E6E06); bias enters through a resistor and/or RF choke on the **output** lead (E6E08 — the distractor swaps input/output); microstrip is the usual MMIC interconnect (E6E07); a typical low-noise UHF preamp noise figure is 0.5 dB (E6E05 — the −10 dB / 44 dBm / −20 dBm distractors are not noise figures at all).
- Packaging: surface-mount parts have the least parasitic inductance/capacitance above HF — smaller area, shorter traces, less parasitics, all true (E6E09, E6E10 — an all-of-the-above); DIP = dual in-line package, two rows of pins on opposite sides (E6E11), the only through-hole part among PLCC/BGA/SOT (E6E02), and its **lead length** is why it disappears at UHF (E6E12 — not dielectric loss).
- Vocabulary: MMIC, GaAs, GaN, microstrip, noise figure, DIP, PLCC, BGA, SOT, surface-mount technology, parasitic inductance/capacitance.

### Beat 6 — Optical devices (E6F)
- The generating side: photons are the particles absorbed in a photovoltaic cell (E6F01); the photovoltaic effect converts light to electrical energy (E6F04); PV efficiency = the fraction of light converted to current (E6F09); silicon is the common power-generating PV material (E6F10); a fully illuminated silicon PV cell gives ≈0.5 V open-circuit (E6F11).
- The sensing side: light **decreases** the resistance of photoconductive material — a crystalline semiconductor is the common one (E6F02, E6F06); an optoisolator is an LED + phototransistor pair giving electrical isolation between a control circuit and a 120 VAC switched circuit (E6F03, E6F08 — isolation, not impedance matching); a solid-state relay implements relay functions in semiconductors (E6F07 — not a transistor driving a mechanical coil); a shaft encoder detects rotation by interrupting a light source with a patterned wheel (E6F05).
- Keep photovoltaic (generates) and photoconductive (changes resistance) apart — the pool tests the boundary.
- Vocabulary: photovoltaic cell/effect, photoconductive, optoisolator/optocoupler, phototransistor, solid-state relay, optical shaft encoder.

## 5. Worked examples (≥1 `> **Worked example:**` blockquote)

- "Identify the device: gate lead enters from the left and touches the channel through a filled arrowhead pointing **away** from the channel; the gate is not insulated. Rule 1: arrow on the gate lead = JFET, not MOSFET. Rule 2: arrow pointing out = P-channel. So symbol 1 in the redrawn Figure E6-1 is a P-channel junction FET (E6A11). Now add a second gate: two insulated gate lines labeled G2 over G1 with the internal arrow pointing in = N-channel dual-gate MOSFET — symbol 4 (E6A10)."
- "How much current can the base-emitter junction tell you about? A forward-biased silicon junction drops about 0.6–0.7 V (E6A07) — so in the redrawn Figure E7-2 (ch07's regulator), where the Zener holds the base at 12.7 V, the emitter sits one junction drop lower at 12 V. One fact, two chapters."
- Package parasitics (Beat 5): "Why does the DIP vanish above HF? Its centimeters of lead length are parasitic inductance (ch05's lesson), so at UHF the 'same' resistor or IC is no longer the same component — surface-mount parts, with millimeter leads and smaller loops, keep their datasheet behavior (E6E12, E6E09)."
