### Beat 1 — RF exposure: what the limits protect (E0A01, E0A02, E0A03, E0A05, E0A06, E0A08)
- Grounding first: the primary function of an external earth connection (ground rod) is **lightning charge dissipation** (E0A01).
- The two populations: a neighbor's home is general-population territory, so your station's signals there must meet the **uncontrolled** MPE limits (E0A02 — controlled limits are for people aware of the exposure, like your own household briefed on the station; the distractors swap "exposure" and "emission").
- The numbers: MPE limits are most restrictive over **30–300 MHz** — the body-resonance region (E0A03); the microwave hazard is that the commonly used high-gain antennas can produce high exposure levels (E0A05); below 300 MHz separate E- and H-field limits exist because the body reacts to both fields AND their intensity peaks can occur at different locations (E0A06 — both clauses true, an all-of-the-above); SAR — specific absorption rate — measures the rate at which RF energy is absorbed by the body (E0A08).
- The citation landscape (canon §7.6): these answers rest on Part 1 territory — §§1.1307/1.1310/2.1091/2.1093 and OET Bulletin 65, pointed to by §97.13(c)(1) — not on Part 97's own text. Say so once; never invent a Part 97 citation for them.
- Vocabulary: MPE, controlled/uncontrolled exposure limits, SAR, E-field/H-field limits, RF exposure evaluation.

### Beat 2 — Evaluations: who must, who is exempt (E0A04, E0A09, E0A10 + the enrichment illustration)
- Multi-transmitter sites: every transmitter producing **5 percent or more** of its MPE limit in an area where the total limit is exceeded shares responsibility for mitigation (E0A04 — the 20% distractor).
- Exemptions: hand-held transceivers sold **before May 3, 2021** are exempt from RF exposure evaluations (E0A09 — not "any transceiver under 7 W," not "dishes under 1 m"); on **80 meters an evaluation must always be performed** (E0A10 — the "always" beats the power/mode-conditional distractors).
- What an evaluation actually looks like (flagged as enrichment — **no E0A question requires this calculation**, canon §2.16/§5): far-field power density S = ERP/(4πR²) — see §5's worked example, then the `{{fig:ch10-mpe-math}}` figure (power × duty × averaging against the MPE limits curve, with the 30–300 MHz region marked most restrictive). Duty cycle and time-averaging lower the effective exposure — a 50% duty conversational SSB transmission is far kinder than a key-down carrier at the same PEP.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch10-mpe-math}}`.
- Vocabulary: RF exposure evaluation, duty cycle, time averaging, power density, aggregate contribution.

### Beat 3 — Tower work: the climbing rules that save lives (E0A07, E0A11, E0A12)
- **100% tie-off**: at least one lanyard is attached to the tower at all times (E0A07).
- Lanyards attach to tower **legs** (E0A11); a shock-absorbing lanyard anchors **above the climber's head level** (E0A12).
- One sentence of craft: the harness, the lanyards, and the discipline are a system — the exam tests the three attachment facts above, and they are also the three things an experienced climber checks before leaving the ground.
- Vocabulary: 100% tie-off, shock-absorbing lanyard, fall arrest.

## 5. Worked examples (≥1 `> **Worked example:**` blockquote — flagged as enrichment)

- "What an MPE check looks like (enrichment — no E0A question requires this calculation): your antenna system radiates 100 W ERP and the neighbor's lot line is 10 m away. Far-field power density S = ERP/(4πR²) = 100/(4π × 10²) ≈ 0.08 mW/cm² — comfortably under the general MPE limits, which run from roughly 0.2 to 1.0 mW/cm² across the HF/VHF range, most restrictive in the 30–300 MHz body-resonance region (E0A03). Then the honest levers: conversational SSB at ~50% duty and 6-minute averaging cut the effective exposure further. The shape of the exercise — ERP out, distance squared in the denominator, duty and averaging applied — is what the FCC means by 'evaluation' (E0A10)."
- Duty-cycle arithmetic (enrichment, same flag): "A 100 W PEP SSB transmission at 50% transmit duty within a 6-minute averaging window presents the thermal load of a 50 W continuous source; key-down modes (RTTY, FT8 at full cycle) get no such discount — one more reason the evaluation is mode-aware in practice."
