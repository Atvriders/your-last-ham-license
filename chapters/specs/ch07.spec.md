### Beat 1 — Digital circuits: flip-flops, counters, gates (E7A)
- The flip-flop is bistable — two stable states (E7A01) — and one stage divides a pulse train by 2, so N cascaded stages divide by 2ⁿ: ÷16 takes 4 flip-flops (E7A04; the 6/8/16 distractors punish guessing anything but 2⁴); a decade counter emits one output pulse per 10 input pulses (E7A02 — it counts; the decoder distractors describe a display driver).
- Multivibrators: astable free-runs between two states with no clock (E7A05); monostable flips for a set time then returns (E7A06); positive logic: high = 1, low = 0 (E7A11).
- Gate truth: NAND is 0 only when all inputs are 1 (E7A07); OR is 1 if any input is 1 (E7A08); XNOR is 0 when exactly one input is 1 — an equality detector (E7A09; option D is the XOR truth, the classic swap); reading truth tables (E7A10).
- Vocabulary: bistable/monostable/astable multivibrator, flip-flop, J-K/T flip-flop, decade counter, truth table, positive logic.

### Beat 2 — Amplifier classes and Figure E7-1 (E7B)
- The conduction-angle ladder — the group's core discrimination: class A conducts the entire 360°, operating point about halfway between saturation and cutoff (E7B04); class AB each push-pull device conducts **more than 180° but less than 360°** (E7B01 — 180° exactly is the class-B distractor); class C less than 180° — efficient but nonlinear, so on SSB it produces signal distortion and excessive bandwidth (E7B07); class D is a switching amplifier, efficient because the device sits at saturation or cutoff most of the time (E7B08) and requires an output filter to remove harmonic content (E7B02, E7B03).
- Practicalities: kill unwanted RF power-amp oscillation with parasitic suppressors and/or neutralization (E7B05); grounded-grid has **low** input impedance (E7B06); the emitter follower (common collector) keeps input and output in phase (E7B09).
- **Reading Figure E7-1** (embed `{{fig:ch07-pool-fig-e71}}`): the common-emitter NPN stage. R1 and R2 form **voltage divider bias** (E7B10); R3 provides **self bias** — it carries the full emitter current, and C3 bypasses it for AC only, so E7B11's "emitter bypass" distractor names C3's job, not R3's (the classic misread — call it out); input at the base, output at the collector, emitter common to both → **common emitter** (E7B12).
- Vocabulary: push-pull, class A/AB/B/C/D, saturation, cutoff, operating point, neutralization, parasitic suppressor, grounded-grid, emitter follower, voltage divider bias, self bias.

### Beat 3 — Filters and matching networks (E7C)
- The network topologies: a low-pass Pi-network = shunt C at input and output with a series L between them (E7C01 — draw it once; the options permute the L/C positions); a T-network with series capacitors and a shunt inductor passes highs (E7C02); a Pi-L adds a series output inductor to a Pi for greater harmonic suppression (E7C03, E7C07); a matching network cancels the reactive part of an impedance and transforms the resistive part to the desired value (E7C04).
- The filter-family ladder (the tested three-way): Butterworth = maximally flat, no ripple; Chebyshev = passband ripple traded for a sharp cutoff (E7C05); elliptical = extremely sharp cutoff **with one or more stop-band notches** (E7C06).
- The specialists: helical filters are the common VHF/UHF band-pass/notch filters (E7C08); a crystal lattice filter is a low-level quartz-crystal filter (E7C09); cavity filters serve repeater duplexers (E7C10); shape factor measures adjacent-channel rejection (E7C11).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch07-active-filters}}`.
- Vocabulary: Pi-network, Pi-L network, T-network, Butterworth, Chebyshev, elliptical filter, helical filter, crystal lattice filter, cavity filter, duplexer, shape factor, passband ripple.

### Beat 4 — Power supplies and regulators, Figure E7-2 (E7D)
- The twin stems the pool cross-quotes: a **linear** regulator varies the conduction of a control element (E7D01); a **switchmode** regulator varies the duty cycle of pulses fed to a filter (E7D02) — and high-frequency switching allows much smaller transformers and filter components, hence lighter and cheaper (E7D10). A Zener is the stable voltage reference (E7D03); a three-terminal regulator is a series regulator (E7D04); a shunt regulator loads the unregulated source (E7D05); dropout voltage = the minimum input-to-output differential needed to stay in regulation (E7D12); a step-start circuit lets filter capacitors charge gradually (E7D15); equal-value resistors across series filter capacitors equalize voltage AND discharge the caps AND provide a minimum load (E7D14 — all three); a solar-panel inverter converts DC to AC (E7D11); battery time = amp-hours ÷ average current (E7D09).
- **Reading Figure E7-2** (embed `{{fig:ch07-pool-fig-e72}}`): +25 in, +12 out. Q1 is the series-pass element — it controls the current to keep the output voltage constant (E7D06 — NOT a chopper; that's the switcher distractor); C2 parallels D1 and bypasses rectifier-output ripple around the Zener, keeping the reference clean (E7D07); the whole thing is a linear voltage regulator (E7D08). The circuit story: D1 holds the base at its Zener voltage, the emitter follows one B-E drop below it (ch06's 0.6–0.7 V), R1 feeds the Zener from the raw rail.
- The same figure's numbers power E7D13's math: P = (V_in − V_out) × I_out = (25 − 12) × 1 = 13 W dissipated in Q1 (worked in §5).
- Vocabulary: pass element, series/shunt regulator, switchmode, duty cycle, dropout voltage, step-start circuit, bleeder/equalizing resistor, inverter, brute-force filter.

### Beat 5 — Modulation and demodulation circuits (E7E)
- Making FM: reactance modulation of a **local oscillator** (E7E01, E7E02 — "of the final amplifier" is the trap); a discriminator detects FM (E7E03).
- Making SSB: a balanced modulator cancels the carrier, a filter removes one sideband (E7E04).
- Emphasis: pre-emphasis boosts the higher audio frequencies at the **transmitter** (E7E05); de-emphasis in the **receiver** restores response and keeps compatibility with phase modulation (E7E06); baseband = the frequency range the message occupies before modulation (E7E07).
- Mixers and detectors: a mixer outputs the two input frequencies plus their sum and difference (E7E08); overdriven mixer inputs generate spurious products (E7E09); the three-detector sort — envelope detector for AM (rectification + filtering, E7E10), discriminator for FM (E7E03), product detector for SSB (E7E11).
- Vocabulary: reactance modulator, frequency discriminator, balanced modulator, product detector, envelope detector, pre-emphasis, de-emphasis, baseband, mixer products.

### Beat 6 — DSP and SDR (E7F)
- Architectures: direct sampling digitizes incoming RF with an ADC — no local oscillator, no mixer (E7F01); the Hilbert-transform (phasing) method generates SSB by combining signals in quadrature (E7F03, E7F04); an adaptive filter removes unwanted noise from a received SSB signal (E7F02 — "adaptive" removes noise, "Hilbert" generates SSB; the distractors swap them).
- Sampling law: Nyquist — sample at least twice the highest frequency component (E7F05); the FFT converts time domain to frequency domain (E7F07); decimation reduces the effective sample rate by removing samples, and the anti-alias low-pass must remove the high-frequency components **first** or they reappear as false low-frequency components (E7F08, E7F09).
- The parameter split the pool tests twice: sample **rate** sets the maximum receive bandwidth (E7F10), while reference voltage and sample width in **bits** set the minimum detectable signal (E7F11). Bits math: 1 V range at 1 mV resolution needs 1,000 codes, and 2¹⁰ = 1,024 ≥ 1,000 → **10 bits** (E7F06; 8 bits = 256 is the trap).
- FIR filters can delay all frequency components equally — linear phase, constant group delay (E7F12); the taps provide the incremental delays, and more taps give a sharper filter (E7F13, E7F14).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch07-sdr-iq}}`.
- Vocabulary: direct sampling, SDR, adaptive filter, Hilbert transform, quadrature, Nyquist rate, FFT, decimation, anti-aliasing filter, FIR, tap, group delay.

### Beat 7 — Op-amps and Figure E7-3: the best return-on-formula in the pool (E7G)
- The device: a high-gain, direct-coupled differential amplifier with very high input impedance and very low output impedance (E7G12, E7G01, E7G03); input offset voltage = the differential input voltage needed to bring the open-loop output to zero (E7G04); gain-bandwidth = the frequency where open-loop gain falls to one (E7G06); an ideal op-amp's gain does not vary with frequency (E7G08); ringing in an op-amp audio filter is prevented by restricting both gain and Q (E7G05).
- **One formula answers five questions** (canon §1.4): Av = −RF/R1, Vout = −(RF/R1)·Vin, read off the redrawn Figure E7-3 (embed `{{fig:ch07-pool-fig-e73}}`). E7G07: 470/10 = **47**; E7G09: −(10,000/1,000) × 0.23 V = **−2.3 V** (sign matters — +2.3 V is the distractor; the other three ask "absolute voltage gain," positive); E7G10: 68 k/1.8 k = 37.8 ≈ **38**; E7G11: 47 k/3.3 k = 14.2 ≈ **14**; a capacitor across RF makes feedback impedance fall with frequency → **low-pass filter** (E7G02). Compute RF/R1 exactly — the distractors are arithmetic slips (24, 4700, 76, 28). All worked in §5.
- Vocabulary: operational amplifier, inverting/non-inverting input, input offset voltage, gain-bandwidth product, open-loop vs closed-loop gain, feedback resistor.

### Beat 8 — Oscillators and frequency synthesis (E7H)
- The oscillator family: the three common circuits are Colpitts, Hartley, and Pierce (E7H01 — Taft/Fenner/Beane are invented); Colpitts feedback = capacitive divider (E7H04 — "tapped coil" is the Hartley distractor); Pierce feedback runs through a quartz crystal (E7H05); a crystal runs on its specified frequency when given the specified parallel load capacitance (E7H12).
- Drift discipline, kept separate: microphonics = frequency changes from mechanical vibration, cured by mechanically isolating the circuit from its enclosure (E7H02, E7H07); NP0 capacitors reduce **thermal** drift (E7H08 — E7H07's distractor raids this answer).
- Synthesis: a PLL is an electronic servo loop — phase detector + low-pass filter + VCO + stable reference — and it can perform frequency synthesis and FM demodulation (E7H03, E7H06). A DDS = phase accumulator + lookup table + DAC + low-pass anti-alias filter (E7H09, E7H10); its spectral impurities are spurious signals at **discrete** frequencies, not broadband noise (E7H11). GPS references, rubidium references, and temperature-controlled dielectric resonators all serve microwave stability (E7H13 — all three).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch07-pll-block}}`.
- Vocabulary: Colpitts, Hartley, Pierce, phase-locked loop, VCO, phase detector, direct digital synthesizer, phase accumulator, lookup table, NP0/C0G, microphonic, load capacitance, dielectric resonator.

## 5. Worked examples (several `> **Worked example:**` blockquotes — pool numbers exactly)

- The E7-3 gain law (mandatory): "Read the redrawn Figure E7-3: gain is the feedback resistor over the input resistor, with a minus sign. RF = 470, R1 = 10: Av = −470/10 = −47, and the pool asks for absolute gain → **47** (E7G07). With 0.23 V in and RF/R1 = 10,000/1,000: Vout = −10 × 0.23 = **−2.3 V** (E7G09 — the minus sign is the answer, +2.3 V the trap). 68 k over 1.8 k = 37.8 ≈ **38** (E7G10); 47 k over 3.3 k = 14.2 ≈ **14** (E7G11). Add a capacitor across RF and the feedback impedance falls as frequency rises → a low-pass filter (E7G02)."
- Regulator dissipation: "In the redrawn Figure E7-2, Q1 drops the raw 25 V to the regulated 12 V while passing the 1 A load current, so it dissipates $P = (V_{in} - V_{out}) \times I_{out}$ = (25 − 12) × 1 = 13 W (E7D13) — the reason that transistor wears a heat sink."
- Battery time: "A 10 Ah battery running a 2 A average load: 10 ÷ 2 = 5 hours (E7D09's formula, operating time = amp-hours ÷ average current)."
- ADC resolution: "An ADC with a 1 V reference that must resolve 1 mV needs 1,000 codes; 2⁹ = 512 is too few, 2¹⁰ = 1,024 covers it → **10 bits** (E7F06). And Nyquist: to reproduce a signal with components to 15 kHz, sample at ≥ 30 kHz (E7F05). Decimate that 1 MS/s stream by keeping every 4th sample → 250 kS/s effective — with everything above 125 kHz filtered out first (E7F08/E7F09's rule)."
- Frequency division: "One flip-flop divides a pulse train by 2; dividing by 16 means 16 = 2⁴, so **4 flip-flops** (E7A04)."
