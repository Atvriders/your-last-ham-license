### Beat 1 — Oscilloscopes, spectrum analyzers, counters, antenna analyzers (E4A)
- The two domain axes to keep straight: an oscilloscope shows amplitude vs **time**; a spectrum analyzer shows amplitude vs **frequency** — the tool for transmitter spurs and intermod products (E4A02, E4A03; the swapped-axes distractor is the classic miss).
- Scope craft: a digital scope's highest accurate frequency is set by its ADC sampling rate — undersampling produces aliasing, a false jittery low-frequency copy (E4A01, E4A06); compensate a ×10 probe on the calibrator square wave until the flat tops are flat (E4A04); keep the probe ground lead short (E4A09); line triggering locks to the AC line — the stable choice for viewing power-supply ripple (E4A10).
- A prescaler **divides** high frequencies into the counter's range (E4A05 — the "multiply" distractor inverts it).
- Antenna analyzers compute SWR and impedance directly and can measure velocity factor, cable length, and tuned-circuit resonance (E4A07, E4A11); SWR is measurable with a directional wattmeter, a VNA, or an antenna analyzer — all three (E4A08, an all-of-the-above).
- Vocabulary: sampling rate, aliasing, probe compensation, trigger modes, spectrum analyzer, prescaler, antenna analyzer, directional wattmeter.

### Beat 2 — Measurement technique: accuracy, S parameters, VNA, power (E4B)
- Accuracy: a counter's accuracy is dominated by its time-base accuracy (E4B01); a meter's ohms-per-volt rating × full-scale voltage = its input impedance — 20 kΩ/V × 10 V = 200 kΩ (E4B02).
- S parameters describe a two-port network, subscripts naming the ports (E4B07): S21 = forward gain — "into port 2 from port 1" (E4B03); S11 = input return loss / reflection coefficient, equivalent to VSWR (E4B04). A two-port VNA measures filter frequency response, input/output impedance, reflection coefficient (E4B09, E4B11); VNA calibration uses short, open, and 50 Ω loads (E4B05).
- Power bookkeeping: absorbed power = forward − reflected — 100 W forward, 25 W reflected delivers 75 W (E4B06; the add-instead-of-subtract 125 W distractor). This wattmeter pair is also the pool's SWR hook: |Γ| = √(P_refl/P_fwd) = √(25/100) = 0.5 → SWR = (1+|Γ|)/(1−|Γ|) = 3:1, return loss ≈ 6 dB — the S11↔VSWR equivalence in action (worked in §5).
- Q from measurement: a series-tuned circuit's Q comes from the bandwidth of its frequency response, Q = f/BW (E4B08 — the inverse of ch05's BW = f₀/Q).
- The transmitter IMD test: two non-harmonically related **audio** tones into the mic input, RF output observed on a spectrum analyzer (E4B10 — the RF-tones distractor describes a receiver test).
- Vocabulary: time base, ohms-per-volt, S parameters (S11/S21/S12/S22), return loss, reflection coefficient, VNA, SOL calibration, forward/reflected power, two-tone IMD test.

### Beat 3 — Receiver performance: noise, selectivity, overload (E4C)
- Noise vocabulary, three distinct terms: noise figure = the dB ratio of receiver noise to the theoretical minimum (E4C04); the reference noise floor is −174 dBm in 1 Hz at room temperature — physics, not a spec (E4C05); MDS = minimum discernible signal (E4C07). Noise scales with bandwidth: 50 Hz → 1,000 Hz costs 10·log₁₀(20) = 13 dB (E4C06).
- Overload mechanisms: reciprocal mixing = LO phase noise mixing with a strong adjacent signal and dumping noise onto the desired one (E4C13 — its distractor defines IMD; keep reciprocal mixing (phase noise × one strong signal) and IMD (nonlinearity × two signals) apart); the same mechanism makes SDR master-clock phase noise dangerous (E4C01); SDR overload = input peaks exceeding the ADC reference voltage (E4C08).
- Selectivity architecture: a high first IF eases image rejection (E4C09); a front-end preselector removes strong out-of-band signals before they can intermod (E4C02); selectable bandwidths match the mode for best SNR (E4C10); a narrow roofing filter improves blocking dynamic range by attenuating strong close-in signals early (E4C12); IF Shift slides the passband away from adjacent interference (E4C14). On the low HF bands front-end attenuation costs almost nothing — atmospheric noise still exceeds internal noise (E4C11). Capture effect: in FM the stronger co-channel signal suppresses the weaker (E4C03).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch04-receiver-performance}}`.
- Vocabulary: noise figure, noise floor, MDS, phase noise, reciprocal mixing, capture effect, blocking dynamic range, roofing filter, preselector, image response, IF Shift, ADC reference voltage.

### Beat 4 — Dynamic range, intermodulation, link budgets (E4D)
- Two different dynamic ranges: **blocking** DR = the dB span from the noise floor to the level causing 1 dB of gain compression — one signal, compression (E4D01; its distractors define the IMD version); poor dynamic range shows up as cross-modulation and desensitization (E4D02); desensitization is tamed by attenuation before the first RF stage (E4D06, E4D07).
- Intermod mechanics: born in nonlinear circuits (E4D08); two close repeaters intermod when their signals mix in a final amplifier (E4D03); a properly terminated circulator on the transmitter output isolates it (E4D04 — the energy coming *back into* the transmitter is the problem path); a preselector raises out-of-band rejection (E4D09). Odd-order products matter because two in-band signals yield odd-order products that also land in-band — 2f1 − f2 (E4D11). IP3 of 40 dBm = the **extrapolated** point where two 40 dBm inputs would produce third-order products equal to the inputs — an extrapolation, not a working level (E4D10).
- Link budgets are pure dB bookkeeping, and this pool drills them three ways (all worked in §5): received power = transmit power + gains − losses − path loss (E4D13); margin = received − MDS − required SNR (E4D12); dBm ↔ watts (E4D14).
- One numbering note: E4D05 was withdrawn by the 4th errata (2026-02-04) and the group was not renumbered — the gap is intentional (canon §1.3).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch04-link-budget}}`.
- Vocabulary: blocking dynamic range, 1 dB compression, desensitization, cross-modulation, intermodulation, IP3, circulator, link margin, path loss, dBm.

### Beat 5 — Noise tools, RFI hunting, grounding (E4E)
- The noise-tool map: noise blanker → impulse noise (ignition, power-line) (E4E03); DSP noise reduction → broadband white, ignition, and power-line noise (E4E02 — all three); the side effects — a blanker can distort strong signals so they *appear* to cause spurious emissions (E4E09), and an automatic notch filter hunting a carrier can notch out your own CW signal (E4E01).
- RFI signatures: network equipment → unstable modulated/unmodulated signals at specific frequencies (E4E06); switch-mode supplies → carriers at regular intervals across a wide range (E4E12); arcing thermostats, doorbell transformers, flickering displays → intermittent roaring/buzzing (E4E10 — all three); corroded metal joints near broadcast sites mix and re-radiate — the rusty-bolt effect (E4E11).
- Cures: alternator/charging noise → ferrite chokes on the charging leads (E4E04); AC-motor RFI → a brute-force AC-line filter in series with the motor leads (E4E05).
- Common-mode current flows equally on all conductors (E4E08) and is what makes shielded cables radiate or pick up interference (E4E07) — chokes kill it without touching the differential signal. Surge protectors mount on the single point ground panel (E4E13), whose job is ensuring all lightning protectors fire simultaneously, preventing lethal chassis-to-chassis potentials (E4E14).
- Vocabulary: noise blanker, DSP noise reduction, automatic notch filter, impulse noise, brute-force line filter, ferrite choke, common-/differential-mode current, switch-mode power supply, rusty-bolt effect, single point ground panel.

## 5. Worked examples (several `> **Worked example:**` blockquotes — this is the dB-bookkeeping chapter; use the pool's own numbers exactly)

- Wattmeter → SWR chain: "Your directional wattmeter reads 100 W forward and 25 W reflected. The load absorbs 100 − 25 = 75 W (E4B06 — not 125 W). The same pair gives the reflection coefficient: |Γ| = √(25/100) = 0.5, so SWR = (1+0.5)/(1−0.5) = 3:1 and the return loss is −20·log₁₀(0.5) ≈ 6.0 dB. One meter, three answers — that's why S11 is 'equivalent to VSWR' (E4B04)."
- Noise vs bandwidth: "Widening the receiver filter from 50 Hz to 1,000 Hz raises the noise floor by $10 \log_{10}(1000/50)$ = 13 dB (E4C06) — against the −174 dBm-in-1-Hz reference floor (E4C05)."
- dBm ↔ watts: "−100 dBm in watts: $P = 10^{((-100-30)/10)}$ = 10⁻¹³ W = 0.1 pW (E4D14)."
- Link budget: "40 dBm transmit power, +6 dB and +3 dB antenna gains, −100 dB path loss: 40 + 6 + 3 − 100 = −51 dBm received (E4D13 — each distractor omits exactly one gain: −54, −57, −60)."
- Link margin: "40 dBm + 10 dB antenna − 3 dB losses − 136 dB path loss = −89 dBm at the receiver; the MDS is −103 dBm, so the signal is 14 dB above the floor; reserve 6 dB of required SNR and the margin is 14 − 6 = +8 dB (E4D12 — the sign-flip distractors punish skipping the SNR reserve)."
- Voltmeter loading: "A 20 kΩ/V meter on its 10 V range presents 20 kΩ/V × 10 V = 200 kΩ to the circuit under test (E4B02)."
