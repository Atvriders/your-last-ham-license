### Beat 1 — Resonance at depth (E5A)
- At resonance X_L = X_C and the reactances cancel. Series RLC: impedance drops to approximately R — minimum impedance, maximum line current, V and I in phase (E5A03, E5A08). Parallel RLC: impedance maximum, input current minimum, but the **circulating** current inside the L–C loop is maximum (E5A06, E5A07 — the "circulating vs input" word swap is the trap). E5A04's keyed "approximately equal to circuit resistance" assumes the parallel-R model — flag the model assumption when teaching; do not "fix" the pool (canon §7.6).
- The resonant-frequency formula used for real: $f_0 = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ — 50 µH + 40 pF → 3.56 MHz (E5A02); 50 µH + 10 pF → 7.12 MHz (E5A10). **R never enters the resonance formula** — both questions hand you a resistor as bait (E5A02's 22.36 MHz distractor is 2π × f₀, the forgot-to-divide slip).
- Q as a circuit property: series Q = X/R, parallel Q = R/X (E5A09); higher Q in a matching network → narrower matching bandwidth (E5A05); half-power bandwidth BW = f₀/Q — 7.1 MHz/150 = 47.3 kHz (E5A11), 3.7 MHz/118 = 31.4 kHz (E5A12). Loaded Q always runs lower than unloaded. Figure: `{{fig:ch05-q-bandwidth}}`.
- Resonant voltage magnification: at series resonance the voltages across L and C can each exceed the applied voltage by ≈ Q (E5A01); raising series Q raises internal voltages (E5A13) — why tank capacitors arc. Worked in §5 with the pool's own 3.56 MHz numbers.
- Vocabulary: resonance, resonant frequency, half-power bandwidth, Q, loaded/unloaded Q, circulating current, resonant voltage rise, matching network.

### Beat 2 — Time constants, admittance, phase (E5B)
- One time constant τ = RC (or L/R): charge to 63.2% of applied or discharge to 36.8% of initial in one τ (E5B01); ≈99.3% at 5τ. Combine components first: E5B04's two parallel 220 µF = 440 µF and two parallel 1 MΩ = 500 kΩ, so τ = 220 s (the 110/440/55 s distractors are the three wrong combinations). Figure: `{{fig:ch05-time-constants-e}}`.
- Admittance: Y = 1/Z = G + jB in siemens — conductance G the real part, susceptance the imaginary part, letter B (E5B02, E5B06, E5B12); polar conversion |Y| = 1/|Z| with the angle **negated** (E5B03); a pure reactance converts to a susceptance of reciprocal magnitude (E5B05).
- Phase memory and arithmetic: ELI — in an inductor voltage leads current by 90° (E5B10); ICE — in a capacitor current leads voltage (E5B09). The series RLC phase angle θ = atan((X_L − X_C)/R): positive = net inductive = voltage leads; negative = capacitive = voltage lags — E5B07 = −14.0° lags, E5B08 = −63° lags, E5B11 = +27° leads (compute X_L − X_C first, then divide by R; each question offers both angles × both directions, and the lead/lag wording is where sign-perfect solvers still lose points).
- Vocabulary: time constant τ, admittance, conductance, susceptance, siemens, ELI/ICE, phase angle.

### Beat 3 — Complex impedance: rectangular, polar, phasors (E5C01–E5C09)
- The j-operator marks the 90° rotation between resistance and reactance — the book uses j (engineering convention, never i), **+j inductive, −j capacitive**. Rectangular Z = R ± jX: a pure capacitive reactance of 100 Ω is 0 − j100 (E5C01); 50 − j25 = 50 Ω resistance in series with 25 Ω capacitive reactance (E5C06).
- Polar = magnitude and phase angle (E5C02): pure inductance sits at +90°, pure capacitance at −90° (E5C03). Conversions: |Z| = √(R² + X²), θ = atan(X/R); back via R = |Z|cos θ, X = |Z|sin θ. Worked in §5: 50 − j25 → 55.9 ∠−26.6° Ω.
- The diagram vocabulary: on rectangular impedance coordinates the horizontal axis is **resistance** and the vertical **reactance** (E5C09); a pure resistance plots on the horizontal axis (E5C07); polar coordinates display magnitude and phase directly (E5C08); a phasor diagram shows phase relationships among impedances at one frequency (E5C05); frequency-response graphs use a logarithmic vertical axis (E5C04).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch05-complex-plane}}`.
- Vocabulary: rectangular/polar coordinates, j-operator, real/imaginary parts, magnitude, phase angle, phasor diagram, logarithmic scale.

### Beat 4 — Figure E5-1: the plotting skill (E5C10–E5C12)

The R, L, and C live in the question stems; the figure is the plane on which the computed impedance is plotted: both axes −600 to +600 Ω, horizontal = resistance, vertical = reactance, eight labeled points.
- The method (teach it as a procedure): compute X_L = 2πfL or X_C = 1/(2πfC), pair with R, plot. E5C10: 400 Ω + 38 pF at 14 MHz → X_C ≈ 300 → 400 − j300 → **Point 4**; E5C11: 300 Ω + 18 µH at 3.505 MHz → X_L ≈ 400 → 300 + j400 → **Point 3**; E5C12: 300 Ω + 19 pF at 21.2 MHz → X_C ≈ 400 → 300 − j400 → **Point 1**. ### Beat 5 — RF effects: skin effect, parasitics, real vs reactive power (E5D + the Γ bridge)
- Skin effect crowds RF current toward the conductor surface, so resistance rises with frequency (E5D01); it is also the primary RF loss in film capacitors (E5D08). Leads are parasitic inductance — keep VHF+ leads short (E5D02); at microwave frequencies short connections also reduce phase shift (E5D04). A conductor's electrical length **increases** with diameter (E5D10 — counterintuitive and keyed that way; pin it as written, mechanism in an aside).
- Parasitics and self-resonance: electrolytics carry too much series inductance for RF (E5D05); an inductor's inter-turn capacitance creates self-resonance (E5D06); self-resonance = nominal + parasitic reactances combining (E5D07); above self-resonance an inductor turns capacitive.
- Real vs reactive power: reactive power means V and I 90° out of phase (E5D03); ideal L/C store energy in fields and return it — nothing is dissipated, "wattless, nonproductive power" (E5D09, E5D12). Only resistance consumes real power: E5D11's 1 A through 100 Ω + j100 Ω dissipates I²R = 100 W — not the 141 W |Z|-based or 200 W arithmetic distractors.
- The reflection-coefficient bridge (one subsection, pointing at ch09): Γ = (Z_L − Z₀)/(Z_L + Z₀), |Γ| = √(P_refl/P_fwd), SWR = (1+|Γ|)/(1−|Γ|), return loss = −20·log₁₀|Γ| — worked once with the E4B06 wattmeter numbers (0.5 → 3:1 ≈ 6 dB). Figure: `{{fig:ch05-swr-gamma}}`.
- Vocabulary: skin effect, parasitic inductance/capacitance, self-resonance, electrical length, real/reactive/apparent power, power factor, reflection coefficient, return loss.

## 5. Worked examples (several `> **Worked example:**` blockquotes — the calculation chapter; pool numbers exactly)

- Resonant frequency: "50 µH and 40 pF: √(LC) = 4.472×10⁻⁸, so $f_0 = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ = 3.56 MHz (E5A02). Swap in 10 pF: 7.12 MHz (E5A10). The 1 MΩ resistor printed in both questions does nothing — R never enters the resonance formula."
- Bandwidth from Q: "A 7.1 MHz signal from a circuit with Q = 150: BW = f₀/Q = 7.1 MHz/150 = 47.3 kHz (E5A11); 3.7 MHz with Q = 118 → 31.4 kHz (E5A12). Read backwards — measure the bandwidth, get the Q (E4B08's trick)."
- Resonant voltage rise: "With E5A02's own numbers: X_L at 3.56 MHz ≈ 1.12 kΩ, so Q ≈ 1118/22 ≈ 51 — and 10 V applied puts ≈ 510 V across the coil or capacitor (E5A01's 'Q times the applied voltage')."
- Time constant: "Two 220 µF capacitors in parallel (440 µF) and two 1 MΩ resistors in parallel (500 kΩ): τ = RC = 440×10⁻⁶ × 500×10³ = 220 s (E5B04). In one τ the capacitor charges to 63.2% of the applied voltage."
- Rectangular → polar: "50 − j25: $|Z| = \sqrt{50^2 + 25^2}$ = 55.9 Ω and θ = atan(−25/50) = −26.6°, so Z = 55.9 ∠−26.6° Ω (E5C06's value). The admittance mirror: |Y| = 1/55.9 = 17.9 mS with the angle negated, +26.6° (E5B03's rule)."
- Series RLC phase: "θ = atan((X_L − X_C)/R): with 250 Ω and 500 Ω reactances against 1,000 Ω, atan((250−500)/1000) = −14.0° — negative, so voltage lags: the circuit is net capacitive (E5B07). The same recipe gives −63° lags (E5B08) and +27° leads (E5B11)."
- The E5-1 plots: "400 Ω in series with a 38 pF capacitor at 14 MHz: $X_C = 1/(2\pi f C)$ ≈ 300 Ω, so Z = 400 − j300 — fourth quadrant, Point 4 on Figure E5-1 (E5C10). 300 Ω with 18 µH at 3.505 MHz: X_L ≈ 400 → 300 + j400 → Point 3 (E5C11). 300 Ω with 19 pF at 21.2 MHz: X_C ≈ 400 → 300 − j400 → Point 1 (E5C12)."
- Real vs reactive power: "1 A flowing through 100 Ω of resistance in series with j100 Ω of reactance: P = I²R = 1 × 100 = 100 W — the reactance adds zero real watts (E5D11); the 141 W and 200 W choices punish using |Z| or blind arithmetic."
- Γ → SWR: "From ch04's wattmeter: |Γ| = √(25/100) = 0.5, so SWR = (1+0.5)/(1−0.5) = 3:1 and return loss ≈ 6.0 dB."
