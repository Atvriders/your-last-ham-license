### Beat 1 — The Extra-only segments and the band-edge math (E1A01–E1A04)
- The prize, mapped exactly: the Extra-only HF slices (3.500–3.525, 3.600–3.800, 7.000–7.025, 7.125–7.175 (R2), 14.000–14.025, 14.150–14.225, 21.000–21.025, 21.200–21.275 MHz — canon §2.1) and the §97.305 emission-segment edges (RTTY/data vs phone/image on 40/30/20/15/10 m). The legacy-Advanced nuance in one sentence (they share parts of the slices). Figure: `{{fig:ch01-extra-band-chart}}`.
- The core skill: legality is judged on where sideband energy falls, not where the dial sits. A carrier-displaying rig's USB signal occupies carrier→carrier+bandwidth; LSB occupies carrier−bandwidth→carrier; §97.307(b) confines emissions to the band or segment available to the control operator.
- Drill all four pool cases: E1A01 (USB at 14.348 MHz reaches 14.351 — the upper 1 kHz is out of the 20 m band); E1A02 (lowest legal LSB display = lower edge + 3 kHz); E1A03 (20 m RTTY/data ends at 14.150, so a 2.8 kHz USB data signal's highest carrier is 14.1472 MHz); E1A04 (LSB at 3.601 MHz reaches ≈3.598, below the 3.600 Extra phone edge — and "not permitted below 3.610 MHz" is false). Worked examples in §5.
- Vocabulary: necessary bandwidth, carrier frequency, suppressed carrier, emission segment.

### Beat 2 — The special bands: 60 m, 2200 m, 630 m (E1A06, E1A07, E1A09, E1C01, E1C03, E1C07, E1C12)
- 60 m **under the current rules** (canon §7.1 — the binding resolution): a contiguous 5351.5–5366.5 kHz segment at 9.15 W ERP **plus four** discrete channels (5332, 5348, 5373, 5405 kHz) at 100 W ERP; CW carrier at the channel center; 2.8 kHz maximum bandwidth everywhere on 60 m. The pool's two surviving 60 m questions remain literally correct: E1A06 (CW at the channel center) and E1C01 (data bandwidth 2.8 kHz). Drill the keyed answers; explain with the current sections (§97.303(h)(3), §97.313(i)) and note E1A06's printed `[97.303(h)(1)]` citation is superseded (preserved verbatim in quotes, never repaired). **Never describe 60 m as "five channels, 100 W ERP" — that rule is dead.**
- 2200 m and 630 m: fixed-location only; 1 W EIRP on 2200 m (E1A07), 5 W EIRP on 630 m except parts of Alaska near Russia (E1A09) — EIRP, so antenna gain counts; the 1/5/50/100 W distractor ladder cross-pairs them. Antennas ≤ 60 m above ground (§97.15(c)).
- The UTC notification dance: before operating 2200/630 m, notify the Utilities Telecom Council (call sign + bands + antenna coordinates), wait 30 days unless told you're within 1 km of PLC systems (E1C03, E1C07) — the utilities were there first. The pool prints "Utilities **Technology** Council" while the rule text reads "Utilities **Telecom** Council" (canon §7.7): pool quotes keep the pool's form, rule quotes the rule's, one sentence may note the discrepancy, never blend them in one quotation.
- The surprise: phone and image are authorized on the **entire** 630 m band (E1C12) — defies HF band-plan intuition; pin it.
- Vocabulary: EIRP, channelized operation, UTC, power-line carrier (PLC), fixed location.

### Beat 3 — Stations in motion and protected places (E1A05, E1A10, E1A11, E1B03–E1B08, E1B11)
- Aboard a US-documented vessel or craft, any FCC amateur licensee (or §97.107 alien reciprocal) may be control operator (E1A05, E1A11) — no special endorsement exists — but the master of the ship or pilot in command must approve the installation (E1A10); the station must be separate from and independent of other radio apparatus.
- Protected places: protect FCC monitoring facilities within 1 mile (E1B03); the National Radio Quiet Zone surrounds the National Radio Astronomy Observatory at Green Bank, WV (E1B05 — the Laurel MD / White Sands / Cape Canaveral distractors are real sites, wrong facility); a 70 cm repeater interfering with government radiolocation must cease or mitigate because 70 cm is secondary there (E1B04).
- Antenna structures: towers >200 ft or near public-use airports may need FAA notification + FCC registration under Part 17 (E1B06); PRB-1 requires state and local zoning to "reasonably accommodate" amateur communications (E1B07, E1B11) — and binds state/local regulation only, **not** homeowners' associations (the classic trap; canon §7.6).
- Broadcast interference with good-design receivers → quiet hours on the offending frequencies (8–10:30 p.m., plus Sunday 10:30 a.m.–1 p.m.), not a shutdown (E1B08).
- Vocabulary: control operator, harmful interference, National Radio Quiet Zone, PRB-1, reasonable accommodation, HAAT.

### Beat 4 — RACES and emergency operation (E1B09, E1B10)
- RACES = any FCC-licensed station certified by the responsible civil defense organization, with a certified control operator (E1B09) — the "club stations"/"all but Technician" distractors misstate the certification requirement.
- RACES stations share all frequencies authorized to the control operator (E1B10); only under the President's War Emergency Powers are they confined to Part 214 segments. One line of §97.403 context (any means in a true emergency) — Book 3 territory, refreshed in one line.
- Vocabulary: RACES, civil defense organization, War Emergency Powers.

### Beat 5 — Emission standards (E1B01, E1B02, E1C09, E1C10, E1F01)
- The definition to memorize cold: a spurious emission is an emission outside the necessary bandwidth whose level can be reduced without affecting the information (E1B01) — the definition contains no dB figure; the "40 dB" distractor borrows from the numeric limits.
- The numbers: below 30 MHz, spurious mean power ≥ 43 dB below the fundamental for post-2003 transmitters (E1C10 — choice A prints `- 43 dB` with an interior space, preserved byte-exact in quotes); 30–225 MHz ≥ 60 dB; no angle-modulated emission below 29 MHz may exceed modulation index 1.0 at the highest modulating frequency (E1C09 — effectively narrowband-only on HF); HF digital voice and SSTV fit a ~3 kHz phone bandwidth (E1B02).
- The data standards: HF RTTY/data = 2.8 kHz authorized bandwidth (the 300-baud cap is pre-December-2023 history — the pool was written to the amended rule; **never resurrect 300 baud as an exam fact**, canon §7.2); VHF 19.6 kilobauds/20 kHz; UHF+ 56 kilobauds/100 kHz.
- Spread spectrum: authorized only above 222 MHz (E1F01 — the 50/144/222/420 MHz ladder), never to obscure meaning, 10 W PEP transmitter limit.
- Vocabulary: spurious emission, necessary bandwidth, mean power, modulation index, angle modulation, spread spectrum.

### Beat 6 — International, reciprocal, and third-party (E1C02, E1C04, E1C06, E1C11, E1F02)
- Foreign contacts are limited to amateur-service remarks of a personal character (E1C02); third-party traffic only where the foreign administration has arrangements with the US (§97.115(a)(2)).
- The map to memorize: CEPT → Europe, IARP → certain countries of the Americas (E1C04, E1C11). CEPT mechanics live in FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048, not Part 97: carry three documents — a copy of the public notice, proof of US citizenship, evidence of the FCC license grant (E1C06). Extra/Advanced get full CEPT privileges; General gets CEPT Novice only. No "/CEPT" suffix, no local-language ID requirement. Figure: `{{fig:ch01-cept-carry}}`.
- Canadian licensees in the US operate on their home license's terms capped at US Extra privileges (E1F02).
- Vocabulary: CEPT, IARP, reciprocal operating authority, third-party communications, DA 16-1048.

### Beat 7 — Special operations: control, space, telemetry, telecommand (E1C05, E1C08, E1D01–E1D12, E1A08, E1F10)
- The definition swap the pool plays deliberately: telemetry = one-way transmission of measurements at a distance (E1D01); telecommand = transmissions that initiate, modify, or terminate functions of a space station (E1D03). Encryption is lawful **only** for space telecommand (E1D02) — the one exception worth pinning.
- The special-station table: space stations must cease on FCC telecommand order; space-station allocations are HF 40/20/15/10 m (WARC bands excluded), VHF 2 m only, UHF 70 cm and 13 cm (E1D07–E1D09 — "6 m and 2 m" feels right and is wrong); any amateur may be an Earth station (E1D11); any station the space-station licensee designates may telecommand (E1D10); model-craft telecommand max 1 W (E1D06); one-way transmissions belong to space, beacon, and telecommand stations (E1D12 — an all-of-the-above).
- On/within 50 km of Earth: a telecommanded station posts all three — license photocopy, licensee label, control-operator label (E1D05 — all-of-the-above); a failed control link must kill transmissions within 3 minutes (E1C08); a radio control link runs through an auxiliary station, whose control operator may be Technician or higher (E1F10 — "General or higher" is the trap).
- Balloon telemetry IDs with its call sign (within 50 km, not a space station — E1D04). Automatic control may carry third-party traffic only in RTTY/data emissions (E1C05). Message-forwarding violations belong to the originating station's control operator (E1A08).
- One dated-rule caution: post-mission disposal for LEO space stations is now **5 years**, not the pool-era 25 (canon §7.2 — not tested, but any satellite-operations prose says 5).
- Vocabulary: telemetry, telecommand, space telecommand station, Earth station, space station, beacon station, one-way communication, auxiliary station, automatic control, remote control, model craft.

### Beat 8 — The volunteer examiner system (E1E)
- The flow: candidate → VE team → VEC → FCC. VEC = an organization with a written FCC agreement to coordinate exams (E1E03); the VECs — not the FCC, not "the ARRL" — maintain the question pools (E1E02); accreditation = a VEC confirming FCC qualifications (E1E04).
- Session conduct: each administering VE is responsible for conduct and supervision (E1E06); a candidate who ignores instructions has their exam terminated immediately (E1E07); no exams for the relatives listed in the rules (E1E08 — employees and friends are fine).
- Paperwork and penalties: failing candidate gets the application back with the grade (E1E05); passing → three VEs certify qualification and compliance (E1E11) and the documents go to the coordinating VEC per its instructions (E1E10) — VEs never issue licenses nor file with the FCC directly (the E1E10/E1E11 citation tags were errata-fixed to `[97.509(m)]`/`[97.509(i)]` — preserve as printed). Reimbursement is out-of-pocket exam costs only — teaching doesn't count (E1E01); fraudulent administration costs the VE their station grant and operator grant (E1E09 — the fine/prison distractors are criminal penalties that don't apply).
- An Extra-class VE can administer all three elements — the same fact ch00 sells as a benefit.
- Vocabulary: VE, VEC, accreditation, administering VE, CSCE, examination element.

### Beat 9 — Prohibited communications, amplifiers, Line A, STA (E1F02–E1F09)
- Business traffic is fine only when neither the amateur nor their employer has a pecuniary interest (E1F07 — the "$25/$50" distractors invent thresholds); communications for hire or material compensation are prohibited (E1F08); encoding to obscure meaning is barred even on a mesh network (E1F09).
- Amplifiers: certification is not required for one built or modified by an amateur for amateur use (E1F03 — its choice D was rewritten by errata 1; the pool-era §97.315(b)(2) waiver is now Reserved); certification testing happens at the lesser of 1500 W or full output (E1F11).
- Line A runs roughly parallel to and south of the US–Canada border; stations north of it may not transmit 420–430 MHz (E1F04, E1F05) — it protects Canadian systems.
- STA = Special Temporary Authority, granted e.g. for experimental amateur communications (E1F06) — §1.931 is the FCC's generic mechanism and never mentions the amateur service; teach the pool answer, present §1.931 as the general mechanism, never cite it as amateur-specific (canon §7.6).
- Vocabulary: pecuniary interest, external RF power amplifier, FCC certification, Line A, STA, mesh network.

## 5. Worked examples (≥1 `> **Worked example:**` blockquote — band-edge arithmetic with the pool's own numbers)

- "Your transceiver displays the suppressed-carrier frequency. On USB the signal occupies the displayed frequency up to displayed + bandwidth: a USB carrier at 14.348 MHz with 3 kHz bandwidth occupies 14.348–14.351 MHz — the 20 m band ends at 14.350 MHz, so the top 1 kHz of your sideband is outside the band even though the dial reads legal (E1A01). On LSB the signal runs the other way: displayed − bandwidth up to displayed, so the lowest legal LSB display is 3 kHz above the band's lower edge (E1A02)."
- "A 2.8 kHz wide USB data signal in the 20 m RTTY/data segment, which ends at 14.150 MHz: the highest legal carrier is 14.150 − 0.0028 = 14.1472 MHz (E1A03). An LSB carrier displayed at 3.601 MHz puts energy down to about 3.598 MHz — below the 3.600 MHz Extra phone edge (E1A04)."
- EIRP sanity check (Beat 2): "EIRP counts antenna gain: 1 W EIRP on 2200 m (E1A07) and 5 W EIRP on 630 m (E1A09) are radiated-power limits, not transmitter-power limits — a gain antenna means less transmitter power, not more reach for free."
