## 1. The exam (Element 4) — pinned

- **FACT:** "Element 4: 50 questions concerning the privileges of an Amateur Extra Class operator license. The minimum passing score is 37 questions answered correctly." — Source: 47 CFR §97.503(c), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.503 (verbatim, verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** The passing threshold is 37 of 50 questions (= 74%, derived arithmetic); the rule pins the count "37 questions answered correctly," not a percentage — print "37 of 50," not "74%," as the authoritative figure. — Source: 47 CFR §97.503(c), eCFR (verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** "Amateur Extra Class operator: Elements 2, 3, and 4" — and "Each applicant must pass an examination for a new amateur operator license grant and for each change in operator class." — Source: 47 CFR §97.501(a) and intro, eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.501 (verbatim, verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** A General upgrading needs Element 4 only: an unexpired (or in-grace-period) General license earns Elements 2 and 3 examination credit, so Elements 2 and 3 are not re-tested. — Source: 47 CFR §97.505(a)(2), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.505 (verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** The 2024–2028 Element 4 exam is built as 50 questions drawn one per group from the pool's 50 groups, out of a 599-question active pool (10 subelements E1–E9 and E0; E7 and E9 reach group letter H). — Source: `canon/ingestion-report.md` §5.1 (parse-authoritative counts, re-verified by parse of `canon/pool-extra.json` 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Every examination question set must utilize questions taken from the applicable published question pool; each pool must contain at least 10 times the number of questions on a single exam and be published and available to the public before use. — Source: 47 CFR §97.507(b) and §97.523 (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** "The administering VEs must give credit to an examinee holding a CSCE for each element the CSCE indicates the examinee passed within the previous 365 days." — Source: 47 CFR §97.505(b), eCFR (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Each CSCE conveying element credit is individually valid for 365 days from its issue date, and no subsequently issued CSCE renews another CSCE's validity period. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual, CSCE section, http://www.arrl.org/files/file/VEs/VE%20Manual%20Web%20Final%202022.pdf (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** To administer an Amateur Extra exam each VE must hold an Amateur Extra Class license ("Amateur Extra Class in order to administer an Amateur Extra Class operator license examination"), in addition to being accredited, at least 18, and never revoked/suspended; every exam is administered by a team of at least 3 VEs at a VEC-coordinated session. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(b)(3)(iii) and (a), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.509 (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-30)

## 2. Upgrade immediacy for Extra — the key delta

- **FACT:** "The person named in an operator license grant of Novice, Technician, General or Advanced Class, who has properly submitted to the administering VEs a FCC Form 605 document requesting examination for an operator license grant of a higher class, and who holds a CSCE indicating that the person has completed the necessary examinations within the previous 365 days, is authorized to exercise the rights and privileges of the higher operator class until final disposition of the application or until 365 days following the passing of the examination, whichever comes first." — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.9 (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** §97.9(b) covers General→Extra upgrades exactly as it covers Technician→General: both conditions (Form 605 properly submitted to the VEs + CSCE in hand) are normally satisfied at the exam session, so the new Extra privileges are legal to use immediately — before the VEC files anything and before ULS changes. — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(b), eCFR (verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** While operating under §97.9(b), "an indicator must be included after the call sign as follows: ... (3) For a control operator who has requested a license modification from Novice, Technician, General, or Advanced Class to Amateur Extra Class: AE." — Source: 47 CFR §97.119(f)(3), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.119 (verbatim, verified 2026-07-30 — the AE indicator is (f)(3); (f)(2) is AG for General)
- **FACT:** "Each indicator must be separated from the call sign by the slant mark (/) or by any suitable word that denotes the slant mark." — Source: 47 CFR §97.119(c), eCFR (verbatim, verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** In practice (one VEC's instructions): on phone, say your call sign followed by the words "temporary" or "Interim" AE or Alpha Echo (e.g., "This is KX9ABC temporary AE"); on CW or digital modes including images, sign KX9ABC/AE; once your upgrade is shown in the FCC ULS database, drop the suffix. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "I just upgraded. How long do I have to wait before I can use my new license privileges?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php (extracted from raw HTML 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** The §97.9(b) interim authority ends at "final disposition of the application" — normally the grant appearing in ULS — or 365 days after passing, whichever comes first; after the grant shows, the authority rests on the license itself: the person named in the grant "is authorized to be the control operator of an amateur station with the privileges authorized to the operator class specified on the license grant." — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(a)–(b), eCFR (verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Contrast with a brand-new licensee: a first-time candidate has no operating authority until the grant appears in the FCC ULS database. The wait-for-the-grant rule applies to new licenses only; an existing licensee upgrading operates immediately under §97.9(b). — Source: FCC, Amateur Radio Service, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service (lifted, verified 2026-07-24; page 403'd to curl 2026-07-30 — re-check in browser before publication)
- **FACT:** If a candidate passes multiple exam elements at one session, the VEC transmits one application to the FCC reflecting the highest license class earned — so a Technician may in principle test straight through to Extra in one session (Elements 2, 3, and 4). — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (re-verified 2026-07-30); element credits per 47 CFR §97.505 (verified 2026-07-30)

## 3. What changes when the grant lands

- **FACT:** You keep your call sign when you upgrade: "The station is reassigned its same call sign upon renewal or modification of its license, unless the licensee applies for a change to a new sequentially assigned or vanity call sign on FCC Form 605." — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems (lifted, re-verified 2026-07-24; page 403'd to curl 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** An amateur service license "is normally granted for a 10-year term." — Source: 47 CFR §97.25 (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)

- **FACT:** Candidates younger than 18 pay a reduced $5 ARRL VEC exam session fee ("The Youth Exam Fee For 2026 is $5.00"), regardless of exam level. — Source: ARRL VEC Exam Fees, http://www.arrl.org/arrl-vec-exam-fees (re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** The FCC's $35 application fee (effective April 19, 2022) still applies per application to new-license, renewal, rule-waiver, and vanity-call-sign applications. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (re-verified 2026-07-30)

## 5. Session logistics (shared process facts)

- **FACT:** The current NCVEC Form 605 is the 2022 edition (HTTP 200, application/pdf). — Source: NCVEC, https://www.ncvec.org/downloads/NCVEC_Form_605_2022.pdf (re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** The NCVEC Quick-Form 605 is the standard exam-session application: the applicant completes Section 1 (email address and FRN are mandatory), the three administering VEs print, sign, and date Section 2, and the form goes to the coordinating VEC — never directly to the FCC. — Source: ARRL, NCVEC 605 Instructions, http://www.arrl.org/605-instructions (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** When an examinee passes, 3 VEs certify that the examinee is qualified for the license grant and the VEs must issue a CSCE; when an examinee fails, the VEs must return the application document and inform the examinee of the grade. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(i), (l) and (j) (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** ARRL VEC retest policy: a failed element may be retaken at the same session only if the team has a different version of that element, has the time/resources/willingness, and the applicant pays an additional test fee; nothing in FCC rules entitles a failed candidate to an immediate retest. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual, "Retesting"; Laurel VEC FAQ (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** Candidates must present one legal photo ID (or two forms of non-photo ID if none), and every applicant must answer the Basic Qualification Question (felony conviction status). — Source: ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/what-to-bring-to-an-exam-session (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** ARRL's exam-session finder ("Find an In-Person or Remote Video Online Exam") locates sessions by ZIP code, is updated daily, and includes non-ARRL VEC sessions. — Source: ARRL, Find an Amateur Radio License Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/find-an-amateur-radio-license-exam-session (HTTP 200 re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** HamStudy.org's session page lists both in-person and remote exam sessions run by many VE teams. — Source: https://hamstudy.org/sessions (HTTP 200 re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Exams are offered both in person and as remote video-supervised online sessions; remote availability depends entirely on the individual VE team (Laurel VEC runs in-person exams only). — Source: ARRL session finder (re-verified 2026-07-30); Laurel VEC FAQ (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** An FRN (10-digit FCC Registration Number, obtained by registering in CORES and available immediately) is required before exam day; a valid email address is mandatory on the application because the FCC sends all correspondence, including the official license copy, by email. — Source: FCC CORES FAQ, https://apps.fcc.gov/cores/html/know.html; ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session (lifted, verified 2026-07-23; never print "free of charge" — see Watch items)

## 7. What Extra opens (the honest privilege summary)

- **FACT:** "The Amateur Extra class license conveys all available U.S. Amateur Radio operating privileges on all bands and all modes." Earning it "requires passing a thorough 50 question examination." — Source: ARRL, Getting Licensed, http://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Per the rules (ITU Region 2), an Amateur Extra control operator is authorized the full amateur HF allocations: 80/75 m 3.500–4.000 MHz; 40 m 7.000–7.300 MHz; 20 m 14.000–14.350 MHz; 15 m 21.000–21.450 MHz; plus all of 160 m, 60 m, 30 m, 17 m, 12 m, 10 m and every band above 30 MHz shared with the other classes. — Source: 47 CFR §97.301(b), (a), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.301 (verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** The Extra-only HF slices (what a General gains, computed as §97.301(b) minus §97.301(d), ITU Region 2): 80 m 3.500–3.525 MHz; 75 m 3.600–3.800 MHz; 40 m 7.000–7.025 and 7.125–7.175 MHz; 20 m 14.000–14.025 and 14.150–14.225 MHz; 15 m 21.000–21.025 and 21.200–21.275 MHz. Everything else on those bands was already General territory. — Source: 47 CFR §97.301(b) vs. §97.301(d), eCFR (tables read 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Honest nuance: holders of the legacy Advanced class already share parts of those slices (R2: 75 m from 3.700, 40 m from 7.025, 20 m from 14.175, 15 m from 21.225), so the slices exclusive to Extra are 3.500–3.525, 3.600–3.700, 7.000–7.025, 14.000–14.025, 14.150–14.175, 21.000–21.025, and 21.200–21.225 MHz — including the DX-heavy bottom of the 20 m phone band (14.150–14.175). — Source: 47 CFR §97.301(b)–(d), eCFR (verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Which emission types go in which segments (phone/image vs. RTTY/data) is set by §97.305 — e.g., 20 m: RTTY/data 14.00–14.15 MHz, phone/image 14.15–14.35 MHz. — Source: 47 CFR §97.305, eCFR (lifted, verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** ARRL publishes graphical frequency-allocation charts by license class — the canonical visual reference for the band-by-band detail. — Source: ARRL, Graphical Frequency Allocations, https://www.arrl.org/graphical-frequency-allocations (HTTP 200, re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** International recognition is a real Extra benefit: under FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048, "When the privileges authorized by the FCC license grant are Advanced or Amateur Extra Class operator privileges, the U.S. citizen is granted CEPT Radio Amateur License privileges, in accordance with CEPT Recommendation T/R 61-01 (as amended). When the privileges authorized by the FCC license grant are General Class operator privileges, the U.S. citizen is granted CEPT Novice Radio Amateur License privileges, in accordance with ECC Recommendation (05)06 (as amended)." — Source: FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048 (released 2016-09-16), https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-16-1048A1.pdf (verbatim, PDF fetched and text-extracted 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** Operating in a CEPT country requires carrying three documents: "a copy of this Public Notice, proof of U.S. citizenship, and evidence of the FCC license grant" — the pool itself tests this (E1C06 answer: "You must have a copy of FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048"); the pool also tests CEPT (E1C11) and IARP (E1C04: "A permit that allows US amateurs to operate in certain countries of the Americas"). — Source: FCC DA 16-1048 (2026-07-30); `canon/pool-extra.json`, questions E1C04, E1C06, E1C11 (NCVEC 2024–2028 pool)

## 8. Series completion — what remains after Extra

- **FACT:** The FCC currently issues new amateur operator licenses in only three classes — Technician, General, and Amateur Extra — so Amateur Extra is the top of the US license structure; there is no higher class and no further exam element beyond Element 4. — Source: 47 CFR §97.501 (three classes with their elements, verified 2026-07-30); five classes exist in the rules but Novice and Advanced are legacy classes no longer issued — 47 CFR §97.9(a) (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** The three-book arc maps one-to-one onto the license ladder: Technician (Element 2, 35 questions, 26 to pass) → General (Element 3, 35 questions, 26 to pass) → Extra (Element 4, 50 questions, 37 to pass). — Source: 47 CFR §97.503(a)–(c), eCFR (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** What remains after Extra is choice, not rank — factual color: (a) vanity call signs: Amateur Extra licensees are eligible for Group A formats (1×2, 2×1, and 2×2 with an A-first prefix), the only group reserved to Extra class; a vanity request is filed through ULS with up to 25 choices and carries the $35 application fee; (b) the license runs 10 years and renews like any other; (c) an Extra licensee may serve as a Volunteer Examiner for all three exam levels, including administering Element 4 itself. — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems (Group A formats; lifted, re-verified 2026-07-24 — page 403'd to curl 2026-07-30, re-check in browser); 47 CFR §97.19(d) (lifted, verified 2026-07-23); ARRL, FCC Application Fee (vanity fee, re-verified 2026-07-30); 47 CFR §97.25 (lifted, verified 2026-07-23); 47 CFR §97.509(b)(3) (verified 2026-07-30)
- **FACT:** No Morse code exam exists for any US license class — the FCC dropped the Morse code requirement effective February 23, 2007; Extra today is a written-exam-only step up. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "Do I have to pass a Morse code exam?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
