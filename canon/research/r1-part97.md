## 1. Extra-class frequency privileges and the band-edge math (§97.301, §97.305 — E1A01–E1A04)

- **FACT:** §97.301 bands apply to "an amateur station located within 50 km of the Earth's surface, within the specified ITU Region, and outside any area where the amateur service is regulated by any authority other than the FCC." — Source: §97.301 preamble (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Amateur Extra HF/MF/LF allocations (all-Regions unless noted): 2200 m 135.7–137.8 kHz; 630 m 472–479 kHz; 160 m 1800–2000 kHz (R1 1810–1850); 80 m 3.500–3.600 MHz; 75 m 3.600–4.000 MHz (R1 3.600–3.800, R3 3.600–3.900); 60 m 5.3515–5.3665 MHz (CURRENT — see W1); 40 m 7.000–7.300 MHz (R1/R3 7.000–7.200); 30 m 10.100–10.150; 20 m 14.000–14.350; 17 m 18.068–18.168; 15 m 21.000–21.450; 12 m 24.890–24.990; 10 m 28.000–29.700. — Source: §97.301(b) table (verbatim edges).
- **FACT:** The Extra-only HF segments (in §97.301(b) but absent from General §97.301(d)) are exactly: 3.500–3.525 MHz, 3.600–3.800 MHz, 7.000–7.025 MHz, 7.125–7.175 MHz (Region 2), 14.000–14.025 MHz, 14.150–14.225 MHz, 21.000–21.025 MHz, 21.200–21.275 MHz — Extra-exclusive spectrum exists only on 80, 75, 40, 20, and 15 meters. — Source: §97.301(b) vs §97.301(d) tables.
- **FACT:** Advanced class sits between Extra and General: 80 m 3.525–3.600, 75 m 3.700–4.000 (R2), 40 m 7.025–7.300 (R2), 20 m 14.025–14.150 and 14.175–14.350, 15 m 21.025–21.200 and 21.225–21.450. — Source: §97.301(c) table.
- **FACT:** "Except as specified elsewhere in this part, an amateur station may transmit a CW emission on any frequency authorized to the control operator." — Source: §97.305(a) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Emission-segment edges from §97.305(c)(3) (HF, verbatim): 75 m phone/image = entire band (3.600–4.000 MHz for Extra in R2); 40 m phone/image 7.125–7.300 MHz (RTTY/data 7.000–7.100 and 7.100–7.125); 30 m RTTY/data entire band (no phone, no image); 20 m RTTY/data 14.00–14.15, phone/image 14.15–14.35; 15 m RTTY/data 21.0–21.2, phone/image 21.20–21.45; 10 m RTTY/data 28.0–28.3, phone/image 28.3–29.7. — Source: §97.305(c)(3)(i)–(xx).
- **FACT:** E1A01 pinned: the 20 m band ends at 14.350 MHz (§97.301(b)); a USB signal with carrier 14.348 MHz and 3 kHz bandwidth extends to 14.351 MHz, so the upper 1 kHz is outside the band (answer D). §97.305(c) table emission types apply "subject to the standards specified in § 97.307(f)," and §97.307(b) requires emissions to be "confined to the band or segment available to the control operator."
- **FACT:** E1A02 pinned: with a carrier-displaying transceiver, the lowest LSB carrier keeping the whole signal in band is 3 kHz above the lower band edge (answer D) — an LSB signal occupies roughly carrier-minus-3 kHz to carrier, and §97.307(b) confines emissions to the band/segment.
- **FACT:** E1A03 pinned: 20 m RTTY/data segment ends at 14.15 MHz (§97.305(c)(3)(ix)); the highest carrier for a 2.8 kHz wide USB data signal is 14.150 − 0.0028 = 14.1472 MHz (answer C). The 2.8 kHz figure is the §97.307(f)(3) authorized bandwidth (see §4).
- **FACT:** E1A04 pinned: the Extra 75 m phone segment starts at 3.600 MHz; an LSB carrier at 3.601 MHz puts sideband energy down to ~3.598 MHz, beyond the segment edge (answer C). US Extra operators ARE permitted phone above 3.600 MHz, so distractor D ("not permitted below 3.610 MHz") is false. — Source: §97.301(b), §97.305(c)(3)(ii).
- **FACT:** "A station in a secondary service must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations in a primary service." — Source: §97.303 preamble (verbatim).

## 2. 60 m, 2200 m, 630 m: power, notification, phone (§97.313, §97.303(g), §97.305 — E1A06, E1A07, E1A09, E1C01, E1C03, E1C07, E1C12)

- **FACT:** "An amateur station must use the minimum transmitter power necessary to carry out the desired communications," and "No station may transmit with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP." — Source: §97.313(a), (b) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** 2200 m (135.7–137.8 kHz) limit: "No station may transmit in the 135.7-137.8 kHz (2200 m) band with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP or a radiated power exceeding 1 W EIRP." — Source: §97.313(k) (verbatim; E1A07 answer C).
- **FACT:** 630 m (472–479 kHz) limit: "No station may transmit in the 472-479 kHz (630 m) band with a transmitter power exceeding 500 W PEP or a radiated power exceeding 5 W EIRP, except that in Alaska, stations located within 800 kilometers of the Russian Federation may not transmit with a radiated power exceeding 1 W EIRP." — Source: §97.313(l) (verbatim; E1A09 answer D "except in some parts of Alaska").
- **FACT:** 2200/630 m operation is fixed-location only; amateurs "shall not operate within a horizontal distance of one kilometer from a transmission line that conducts a power line carrier (PLC) signal" in those bands. — Source: §97.303(g)(1) (verbatim in part; E1C03 context).
- **FACT:** Before operating on 2200 or 630 m, operators "shall notify the Utilities Telecom Council (UTC) of their intent to operate by submitting their call signs, intended band or bands of operation, and the coordinates of their antenna's fixed location. Amateur stations will be permitted to commence operations after the 30-day period unless UTC notifies the station that its fixed location is located within one kilometer of PLC systems operating in the same or overlapping frequencies." — Source: §97.303(g)(2) (verbatim; E1C03 answer B, E1C07 answer D).
- **FACT:** On 630 m, phone and image emissions are authorized on the ENTIRE band (472–479 kHz). — Source: §97.305(c)(2)(ii) (table row "630 m | Entire band | Phone, image"; E1C12 answer D).
- **FACT:** "Antennas used to transmit in the 2200 m and 630 m bands must not exceed 60 meters in height above ground level." — Source: §97.15(c) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** (CURRENT 60 m channels) "In the 5330.5-5406.4 kHz band (60 m band), amateur stations may transmit only in the 5351.5-5366.5 kHz band and on the four center frequencies specified in the table below" — centers 5332, 5348, 5373, 5405 kHz; "For CW emissions (emission designator 150HA1A), the carrier frequency is set to the center frequency." — Source: §97.303(h)(3) (verbatim; E1A06 answer B remains correct under current text). See W1.
- **FACT:** [POOL-ERA] At 2024-07-01, §97.303(h)(1) authorized 60 m operation "only on the five center frequencies" 5332, 5348, 5358.5, 5373, 5405 kHz, with the same CW-at-center rule. — Source: §97.303(h)(1) as of 2024-07-01. See W1.
- **FACT:** (CURRENT 60 m power) "No station may transmit on the frequencies 5.332, 5.348, 5.373, and 5.405 MHz in the 60 m band with a radiated power exceeding 100 W ERP. No station may transmit in the 5.3515-5.3665 MHz band with a radiated power exceeding 9.15 W ERP." — Source: §97.313(i) (verbatim, amended Jan 14, 2026; ERP = PEP × dipole-relative gain, dipole = 0 dBd presumed). Not tested by E1. See W1.

## 3. Stations aboard ships or aircraft (§97.5, §97.11 — E1A05, E1A10, E1A11)

- **FACT:** "The station apparatus must be under the physical control of a person named in an amateur station license grant on the ULS consolidated license database or a person authorized for alien reciprocal operation by § 97.107 of this part" before transmitting from places including "Within 50 km of the Earth's surface and aboard any vessel or craft that is documented or registered in the United States" and "More than 50 km above the Earth's surface aboard any craft that is documented or registered in the United States." — Source: §97.5(a)(1)–(3) (verbatim; E1A05 answer C).
- **FACT:** No special ship/aircraft endorsement exists in Part 97: any holder of an FCC amateur operator/primary grant (or §97.107 alien reciprocal authority) may operate aboard a US-registered vessel in international waters. — Source: §97.5(a), (c) (basis of E1A11 answer B).
- **FACT:** "The installation and operation of an amateur station on a ship or aircraft must be approved by the master of the ship or pilot in command of the aircraft." — Source: §97.11(a) (verbatim; E1A10 answer A).
- **FACT:** The shipboard/aircraft station "must be separate from and independent of all other radio apparatus installed on the ship or aircraft, except a common antenna may be shared with a voluntary ship radio installation," and must not constitute a hazard to safety of life or property (aircraft under IFR: must comply with FAA rules). — Source: §97.11(b), (c) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** "When transmitting, each amateur station must have a control operator" who holds an operator/primary grant on the ULS or §97.107 alien reciprocal authority. — Source: §97.7 (verbatim in part).

## 4. Emission standards (§97.307, §97.309, §97.311 — E1B01, E1B02, E1C09, E1C10, E1F01)

- **FACT:** Spurious emission = "An emission, or frequencies outside the necessary bandwidth of a transmission, the level of which may be reduced without affecting the information being transmitted." — Source: §97.3(a)(43) (verbatim; E1B01 answer D — the definition contains no dB figure; the "40 dB" distractor borrows from §97.307's numeric limits).
- **FACT:** Bandwidth = "The width of a frequency band outside of which the mean power of the transmitted signal is attenuated at least 26 dB below the mean power of the transmitted signal within the band." — Source: §97.3(a)(8) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** "No amateur station transmission shall occupy more bandwidth than necessary for the information rate and emission type being transmitted, in accordance with good amateur practice." — Source: §97.307(a) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Spurious limit below 30 MHz: for transmitters installed after January 1, 2003, "the mean power of any spurious emission from a station transmitter or external RF power amplifier transmitting on a frequency below 30 MHz must be at least 43 dB below the mean power of the fundamental emission." — Source: §97.307(d) (verbatim; E1C10 answer A, −43 dB). Pre-2003 transmitters: ≤50 mW and ≥40 dB down; <5 W pre-2003: ≥30 dB; pre-April 15, 1977 / pre-1978-marketed exempt.
- **FACT:** Spurious limit 30–225 MHz: "at least 60 dB below the mean power of the fundamental"; transmitters of mean power ≤25 W: spurious at the antenna line ≤25 µW and ≥40 dB down, "but need not be reduced below the power of 10 µW." — Source: §97.307(e) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** "No angle-modulated emission may have a modulation index greater than 1 at the highest modulation frequency." — Source: §97.307(f)(1) (verbatim; E1C09 answer B — this standard applies where (f)(1) is cited, i.e. phone/image segments below 29 MHz).
- **FACT:** "No non-phone emission shall exceed the bandwidth of a communications quality phone emission of the same modulation type." — Source: §97.307(f)(2) (verbatim; basis of E1B02 answer A — HF digital voice and SSTV must fit a ~3 kHz phone bandwidth).
- **FACT:** (CURRENT data standard) "Only a RTTY or data emission using a specified digital code listed in § 97.309(a) may be transmitted. The authorized bandwidth is 2.8 kHz except in the 2200 m band and 630 m band. In the 2200 m band and the 630 m band the symbol rate must not exceed 300 bauds, or for frequency-shift keying, the frequency shift between mark and space must not exceed 1 kHz." — Source: §97.307(f)(3) (verbatim; (f)(4) is Reserved).
- **FACT:** [PRE-POOL history] Before Dec 7, 2023 (88 FR 85127), (f)(3) capped HF data at "300 bauds" (or 1 kHz FSK shift) and (f)(4) capped 10 m data at "1200 bauds." The pool-era (2024-07-01) text already carried the 2.8 kHz standard, so the pool was written to the amended rule (E1A03's "2.8 kHz wide USB data signal", E1C01 answer D). — Source: §97.307(f)(3),(4) as of 2023-07-01 vs 2024-07-01.
- **FACT:** VHF data standard: symbol rate ≤19.6 kilobauds, authorized bandwidth 20 kHz ((f)(5)); UHF-and-up: ≤56 kilobauds, 100 kHz ((f)(6)). — Source: §97.307(f)(5), (f)(6) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** 60 m emissions (CURRENT): "A station may transmit only phone, RTTY, data, and CW emissions. RTTY or data emissions must meet the digital code specifications listed in § 97.309. Emissions must not exceed a bandwidth of 2.8 kilohertz." — Source: §97.307(f)(14)(i) (verbatim; E1C01 answer D "2.8 kHz" correct under both pool-era and current text). See W1.
- **FACT:** Publicly documented digital techniques: a station transmitting RTTY/data with a specified code "may use any technique whose technical characteristics have been documented publicly, such as CLOVER, G-TOR, or PacTOR, for the purpose of facilitating communications"; unspecified codes "must not be transmitted for the purpose of obscuring the meaning of any communication." — Source: §97.309(a)(4), (b) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Spread spectrum (SS) first appears in the §97.305(c) table at the 1.25 m row "222-225 MHz … SS" and then in every UHF/SHF/EHF row — SS is authorized only on amateur frequencies above 222 MHz (never on 6 m or 2 m). — Source: §97.305(c)(4)(v), (c)(5)–(7) (E1F01 answer B).
- **FACT:** SS transmissions are authorized "only for communications between points within areas where the amateur service is regulated by the FCC" (plus countries that permit SS) and "must not be used for the purpose of obscuring the meaning of any communication"; a Regional Director may order SS stopped/restricted and require records convertible to the original information. — Source: §97.311(a)–(c) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** SS power: "No station may transmit with a transmitter output exceeding 10 W PEP when the station is transmitting a SS emission type." — Source: §97.313(j) (verbatim).

## 5. Station location, antennas, interference (§97.13, §97.15, §97.121, §97.303 — E1B03–E1B08, E1B11, E1F04, E1F05)

- **FACT:** "A station within 1600 m (1 mile) of an FCC monitoring facility must protect that facility from harmful interference," on pain of operating restrictions under §97.121; facility coordinates are in §0.121(c). — Source: §97.13(b) (verbatim in part; E1B03 answer A).
- **FACT:** "Owners of certain antenna structures more than 60.96 meters (200 feet) above ground level at the site or located near or at a public use airport must notify the Federal Aviation Administration and register with the Commission as required by part 17 of this chapter." — Source: §97.15(a) (verbatim; E1B06 answer A).
- **FACT:** "State and local regulation of a station antenna structure must not preclude amateur service communications. Rather, it must reasonably accommodate such communications and must constitute the minimum practicable regulation to accomplish the state or local authority's legitimate purpose. See PRB-1, 101 FCC 2d 952 (1985) for details." — Source: §97.15(b) (verbatim; E1B07 answer C "state and local zoning", E1B11 answer B "reasonable accommodations"). PRB-1 is an FCC declaratory ruling referenced by the rule, not itself a CFR section (see W8).
- **FACT:** Restricted operation for broadcast interference: if an amateur station causes general interference to domestic broadcast reception "when receivers of good engineering design … are used," it "shall not be operated during the hours from 8 p.m. to 10:30 p.m., local time, and on Sunday for the additional period from 10:30 a.m. until 1 p.m., local time, upon the frequency or frequencies used when the interference is created." — Source: §97.121(a) (verbatim; E1B08 answer D — quiet hours on the interfering frequencies, not a full ban).
- **FACT:** Line A "Begins at Aberdeen, WA, running by great circle arc to the intersection of 48° N, 120° W, thence along parallel 48° N, to the intersection of 95° W, thence by great circle arc through the southernmost point of Duluth, MN …" — i.e., roughly parallel to and south of the US–Canada border. — Source: §97.3(a)(30) (verbatim opening; E1F04 answer A).
- **FACT:** "No amateur station shall transmit from north of Line A in the 420-430 MHz segment." — Source: §97.303(m)(1) (verbatim; E1F05 answer D).
- **FACT:** National Radio Quiet Zone = "The area in Maryland, Virginia and West Virginia Bounded by 39°15′ N on the north, 78°30′ W on the east, 37°30′ N on the south and 80°30′ W on the west." — Source: §97.3(a)(33) (verbatim; E1B05 answer C — it surrounds the National Radio Astronomy Observatory, Green Bank, WV; §97.203(e) requires written notification to the NRAO Interference Office before establishing an automatically controlled beacon there).
- **FACT:** "Amateur stations transmitting in the 70 cm band, the 33 cm band, the 23 cm band, the 5 cm band, the 3 cm band, or the 24.05-24.25 GHz segment must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations authorized by the United States Government in the radiolocation service." — Source: §97.303(b) (verbatim; basis of E1B04 answer C — a 70 cm repeater interfering with radiolocation must cease or mitigate, because the amateur allocation is secondary).

## 6. Special operations: auxiliary, repeater, beacon, space, Earth, telecommand, forwarding, automatic digital (§§97.201–97.221, 97.115 — E1A08, E1C05, E1C08, E1D01–E1D12, E1F10)

- **FACT:** Auxiliary station: "Any amateur station licensed to a holder of a Technician, General, Advanced or Amateur Extra Class operator license may be an auxiliary station," and such a holder "may be the control operator of an auxiliary station, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held." — Source: §97.201(a) (verbatim; E1F10 answer B).
- **FACT:** "An auxiliary station may transmit only on the 2 m and shorter wavelength bands, except the 144.0-144.5 MHz, 145.8-146.0 MHz, 219-220 MHz, 222.00-222.15 MHz, 431-433 MHz, and 435-438 MHz segments"; it "may be automatically controlled" and "may transmit one-way communications." — Source: §97.201(b), (d), (e) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** Repeater: may operate "only on the 10 m and shorter wavelength frequency bands except the 28.0-29.5 MHz, 50.0-51.0 MHz, 144.0-144.5 MHz, 145.5-146.0 MHz, 222.00-222.15 MHz, 431.0-433.0 Mhz, and 435.0-438.0 Mhz segments"; "A repeater may be automatically controlled"; "The control operator of a repeater that retransmits inadvertently communications that violate the rules in this part is not accountable for the violative communications." — Source: §97.205(b), (d), (g) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** Beacon: transmitter power "must not exceed 100 W"; automatic control only on 28.20–28.30, 50.06–50.08, 144.275–144.300, 222.05–222.06, 432.300–432.400 MHz or 33 cm and shorter; "A beacon may transmit one-way communications." — Source: §97.203(c), (d), (g) (verbatim in part; NRQZ notification §97.203(e) above).
- **FACT:** "A space station must be capable of effecting a cessation of transmissions by telecommand whenever such cessation is ordered by the FCC." — Source: §97.207(b) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Space-station authorized frequencies: "The 17 m, 15 m, 12 m, and 10 m bands, 6 mm, 4 mm, 2 mm and 1 mm bands; and … the 7.0-7.1 MHz, 14.00-14.25 MHz, 144-146 MHz, 435-438 MHz, 2400-2450 MHz, 5.83-5.85 GHz, 10.45-10.50 GHz, and 24.00-24.05 GHz segments." — Source: §97.207(c) (verbatim). This pins E1D07: among the choices only "40, 20, 15, 10 m" (answer A) is fully authorized (7.0–7.1, 14.00–14.25, 15 m and 10 m rows; 17/12 m are also listed but appear in no choice; 30 m is NOT listed, killing distractor B), E1D08 (only VHF band listed is 144–146 MHz = 2 m; answer D), E1D09 (70 cm 435–438 and 13 cm 2400–2450 listed; 33 cm not listed; answer B).
- **FACT:** "A space station may transmit one-way communications," may "automatically retransmit the radio signals of Earth stations and other space stations," and space telemetry "may consist of specially coded messages intended to facilitate communications or related to the function of the spacecraft." — Source: §97.207(d), (e), (f) (verbatim in part; (e) is half of E1D12's citation).
- **FACT:** "Any amateur station may be an Earth station. A holder of any class operator license may be the control operator of an Earth station, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held by the control operator." — Source: §97.209(a) (verbatim; E1D11 answer D).
- **FACT:** "Any amateur station designated by the licensee of a space station is eligible to transmit as a telecommand station for that space station, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held by the control operator." — Source: §97.211(a) (verbatim; E1D10 answer B).
- **FACT:** "A telecommand station may transmit special codes intended to obscure the meaning of telecommand messages to the station in space operation" — the only amateur transmission lawfully encrypted. — Source: §97.211(b) (verbatim; E1D02 answer B). A telecommand station "may transmit one-way communications" (§97.211(d); with §97.203(g) and §97.207(e) pins E1D12 answer A: space, beacon, telecommand).
- **FACT:** Telecommand of a station on/within 50 km of Earth's surface requires: a radio or wireline control link ("If radio, the control link must use an auxiliary station"); "Provisions … to limit transmission by the station to a period of no more than 3 minutes in the event of malfunction in the control link"; protection against unauthorized transmissions; and "A photocopy of the station license and a label with the name, address, and telephone number of the station licensee and at least one designated control operator … posted in a conspicuous place at the station location." — Source: §97.213(a)–(d) (verbatim in part; E1C08 answer B "3 minutes", E1D05 answer D "all these choices").
- **FACT:** Model-craft telecommand: station ID not required for transmissions directed only to the model craft if a call-sign/licensee label is affixed to the transmitter; control signals are not codes/ciphers; "The transmitter power must not exceed 1 W." — Source: §97.215(a)–(c) (verbatim in part; E1D06 answer A).
- **FACT:** "Telemetry transmitted by an amateur station on or within 50 km of the Earth's surface is not considered to be codes or ciphers intended to obscure the meaning of communications." — Source: §97.217 (verbatim). Telemetry = "A one-way transmission of measurements at a distance from the measuring instrument." — Source: §97.3(a)(46) (verbatim; E1D01 answer A).
- **FACT:** Telecommand station (the §97.3(a)(45) the pool cites for E1D03) = "An amateur station that transmits communications to initiate, modify or terminate functions of a space station." — Source: §97.3(a)(45) (verbatim; E1D03 answer B).
- **FACT:** Station identification: "Each amateur station, except a space station or telecommand station, must transmit its assigned call sign on its transmitting channel at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication…." — Source: §97.119(a) (verbatim in part; E1D04 answer A — a balloon-borne telemetry station is within 50 km of Earth, hence NOT a space station, so the call sign IS required; the other choices are not).
- **FACT:** Message forwarding: "the control operator of the station originating a message is primarily accountable for any violation of the rules in this part contained in the message"; forwarding stations that inadvertently retransmit violations are not accountable, except the first forwarding station must authenticate the sender or accept accountability. — Source: §97.219(b)–(d) (verbatim in part; E1A08 answer B).
- **FACT:** Automatically controlled digital stations: permitted on the 6 m and shorter bands and on segments 28.120–28.189, 24.925–24.930, 21.090–21.100, 18.105–18.110, 14.0950–14.0995, 14.1005–14.112, 10.140–10.150, 7.100–7.105, 3.585–3.600 MHz; elsewhere a station may be automatically controlled only when "responding to interrogation by a station under local or remote control" with bandwidth ≤500 Hz (60 m channels excluded, §97.303(h)). — Source: §97.221(a)–(c) (verbatim in part; E1C05's [97.221(c)(1)] half).
- **FACT:** "No station may transmit third party communications while being automatically controlled except a station transmitting a RTTY or data emission." — Source: §97.115(c) (verbatim; E1C05 answer B).

## 7. RACES and emergency operation (§97.401–§97.407 — E1B09, E1B10)

- **FACT:** "No station may transmit in RACES unless it is an FCC-licensed primary, club, or military recreation station and it is certified by a civil defense organization as registered with that organization. No person may be the control operator of an amateur station transmitting in RACES unless that person holds a FCC-issued amateur operator license and is certified by a civil defense organization as enrolled in that organization." — Source: §97.407(a) (verbatim; E1B09 answer C — any FCC-licensed amateur station certified by the responsible civil defense organization).
- **FACT:** "The frequency bands and segments and emissions authorized to the control operator are available to stations transmitting communications in RACES on a shared basis with the amateur service." Only if the President's War Emergency Powers (47 U.S.C. 606) are invoked are RACES stations confined to segments authorized under Part 214. — Source: §97.407(b) (verbatim in part; E1B10 answer A).
- **FACT:** RACES stations may communicate only upon authorization of the responsible civil defense official, with (1) amateur stations registered with the same or another civil defense organization and (2) FCC-regulated services when authorized by the FCC; permissible content includes messages concerning public safety/national defense during emergencies and relief. — Source: §97.407(c), (d) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** "No provision of these rules prevents the use by an amateur station of any means of radiocommunication at its disposal to provide essential communication needs in connection with the immediate safety of human life and immediate protection of property when normal communication systems are not available." — Source: §97.403 (verbatim). Stations in distress may use any means to attract attention (§97.405); Alaska stations may use J3E/R3E on 5.1675 MHz for emergency communications, ≤150 W PEP (§97.401).

## 8. International, reciprocal, third-party (§97.107, §97.117, §97.115, §97.5(d)(e), §97.3 — E1C02, E1C04, E1C06, E1C11, E1F02)

- **FACT:** Canadian-licensed operators in the US get: the terms of the US–Canada Convention (TIAS No. 2508), "The operating terms and conditions of the amateur service license issued by the Government of Canada," and the rules of Part 97, "but not to exceed the control operator privileges of an FCC-granted Amateur Extra Class operator license." — Source: §97.107(a) (verbatim in part; E1F02 answer C). Same ceiling for other reciprocal countries (§97.107(b)); FCC may modify/suspend/cancel (§97.107(c)).
- **FACT:** "Transmissions to a different country, where permitted, shall be limited to communications incidental to the purposes of the amateur service and to remarks of a personal character." — Source: §97.117 (verbatim; E1C02 answer C).
- **FACT:** Third-party messages to foreign stations are allowed only where the foreign administration "has made arrangements with the United States to allow amateur stations to be used for transmitting international communications on behalf of third parties," or for emergency/disaster relief; the prohibition does not apply to a message for a third party eligible to be the station's control operator. — Source: §97.115(a)(2) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** CEPT radio amateur license = "A license issued by a country belonging to the European Conference of Postal and Telecommunications Administrations (CEPT) that has adopted Recommendation T/R 61-01 (Nice 1985, Paris 1992, Nicosia 2003)." — Source: §97.3(a)(12) (verbatim; E1C11 answer A — CEPT is the arrangement letting US amateurs operate in many European countries and vice versa).
- **FACT:** A CEPT radio-amateur license (like an IARP) is issued "by the country of which the person is a citizen," and the holder must not be a US citizen/resident alien, must not hold an FCC amateur grant, and must not be under FCC enforcement sanction. — Source: §97.5(d), (e) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** IARP = "A document issued pursuant to the terms of the Inter-American Convention on an International Amateur Radio Permit by a country signatory to that Convention, other than the United States. Montrouis, Haiti. AG/doc.3216/95." — Source: §97.3(a)(24) (verbatim; E1C04 answer A — a permit for operation in certain countries of the Americas).
- **FACT:** CEPT mechanics are NOT in Part 97: under FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048 ("Amateur Service Operation in CEPT Countries," Sept 16, 2016), a US Extra or Advanced operator (CEPT Class 1) operating in a CEPT country must carry a copy of that Public Notice, proof of US citizenship, and evidence of the FCC license grant (official, not reference copy). — Source: DA 16-1048, confirmed via [FCC amateur releases page](https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service) and [ARRL CEPT guidance](http://www.arrl.org/cept) (E1C06 answer C "copy of FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048"). No "/CEPT" suffix and no local-language ID requirement exists in these documents (distractors A, D false).

## 9. Prohibited transmissions and pecuniary interest (§97.113 — E1F07, E1F08, E1F09)

- **FACT:** "No amateur station shall transmit: … Communications for hire or for material compensation, direct or indirect, paid or promised, except as otherwise provided in these rules." — Source: §97.113(a)(2) (verbatim; E1F08 answer A).
- **FACT:** Also prohibited: "Communications in which the station licensee or control operator has a pecuniary interest, including communications on behalf of an employer," with exceptions for employer disaster drills (1 hr/week, 72 hr twice/year), occasional apparatus sale notices, paid teachers using the station in classroom instruction, and club-station telegraphy-practice/bulletin control operators. — Source: §97.113(a)(3)(i)–(iv) (verbatim in part; E1F07 answer D — a business message only when neither the amateur nor the employer has a pecuniary interest).
- **FACT:** Also prohibited: "Music using a phone emission except as specifically provided elsewhere in this section; communications intended to facilitate a criminal act; messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning, except as otherwise provided herein; obscene or indecent words or language; or false or deceptive messages, signals or identification." — Source: §97.113(a)(4) (verbatim; E1F09 answer C — encrypted/obscuring messages cannot transit a mesh network; email and third-party traffic are not per-se prohibited).
- **FACT:** "An amateur station shall not engage in any form of broadcasting, nor may an amateur station transmit one-way communications except as specifically provided in these rules…." — Source: §97.113(b) (verbatim in part).

## 10. Examinations, VEs, VECs (§97.501–§97.527, §97.9 — E1E01–E1E11)

- **FACT:** Element requirements: Extra = Elements 2, 3, and 4; General = 2 and 3; Technician = 2. — Source: §97.501 (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** "Element 4: 50 questions concerning the privileges of an Amateur Extra Class operator license. The minimum passing score is 37 questions answered correctly." (Elements 2/3: 35 questions, 26 to pass.) — Source: §97.503 (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** "All VECs must cooperate in maintaining one question pool for each written examination element. Each question pool must contain at least 10 times the number of questions required for a single examination. Each question pool must be published and made available to the public prior to its use for making a question set." — Source: §97.523 (verbatim; E1E02 answer C — the VECs, not the FCC or ARRL, maintain the pools).
- **FACT:** VEC = an organization that "has entered into a written agreement with the FCC," exists "for the purpose of furthering the amateur service," and agrees to coordinate exams for any operator class without discrimination. — Source: §97.521 (verbatim in part; E1E03 answer C). [Watch: former §97.521(b) VEC-region requirement is Reserved and Appendix 2 "VEC Regions" deleted — 90 FR 57712, Dec 12, 2025; W3.]
- **FACT:** Accreditation: "A VEC must confirm that the VE applicant meets FCC requirements to serve as an examiner" is the operational reading of §97.525 — no VEC may accredit a person who fails minimum qualifications, whose services the FCC won't accept, whom the VEC finds incompetent, or whose integrity is in doubt. — Source: §97.525(a) (verbatim in part; E1E04 answer D).
- **FACT:** Each exam "must be administered by a team of at least 3 VEs at an examination session coordinated by a VEC." — Source: §97.509(a) (verbatim). Administering VEs must be VEC-accredited, ≥18, hold the required class (Extra for Element 4 sessions; Extra/Advanced for General; Extra/Advanced/General for Technician), and must never have had an amateur station or operator license revoked or suspended. — Source: §97.509(b) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** "Each administering VE must observe the examinee throughout the entire examination. The administering VEs are responsible for the proper conduct and necessary supervision of each examination. The administering VEs must immediately terminate the examination upon failure of the examinee to comply with their instructions." — Source: §97.509(c) (verbatim; E1E06 answer C, E1E07 answer B; §97.511: "Each examinee must comply with the instructions given by the administering VEs.").
- **FACT:** "No VE may administer an examination to his or her spouse, children, grandchildren, stepchildren, parents, grandparents, stepparents, brothers, sisters, stepbrothers, stepsisters, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews, and in-laws." — Source: §97.509(d) (verbatim; E1E08 answer C).
- **FACT:** "No VE may administer or certify any examination by fraudulent means or for monetary or other consideration including reimbursement in any amount in excess of that permitted. Violation of this provision may result in the revocation of the grant of the VE's amateur station license and the suspension of the grant of the VE's amateur operator license." — Source: §97.509(e) (verbatim; E1E09 answer A).
- **FACT:** Grading and certification: VEs grade immediately (remote exams: earliest practical opportunity) and are "responsible for determining the correctness of the examinee's answers" (§97.509(h)); when all elements are credited, "3 VEs must certify that the examinee is qualified for the license grant and that the VEs have complied with these administering VE requirements," and they are "jointly and individually accountable" (§97.509(i), verbatim — E1E11 answer B).
- **FACT:** "When the examinee does not score a passing grade on an examination element, the administering VEs must return the application document to the examinee and inform the examinee of the grade." — Source: §97.509(j) (verbatim; E1E05 answer B). Passing examinees get a CSCE (§97.509(l)).
- **FACT:** "After the administration of a successful examination for an amateur operator license, the administering VEs must submit the application document to the coordinating VEC according to the coordinating VEC's instructions." — Source: §97.509(m) (verbatim; E1E10 answer C). The VEC screens, resolves discrepancies, and forwards data electronically to the FCC, retaining records ≥15 months (§97.519(b)).
- **FACT:** "VEs and VECs may be reimbursed by examinees for out-of-pocket expenses incurred in preparing, processing, administering, or coordinating an examination for an amateur operator license." — Source: §97.527 (verbatim; E1E01 answer A — teaching and study materials are NOT reimbursable).
- **FACT:** Instant upgrade: a holder of a Novice/Technician/General/Advanced grant with a CSCE for the higher-class element(s) completed within the previous 365 days "is authorized to exercise the rights and privileges of the higher operator class until final disposition of the application or until 365 days following the passing of the examination, whichever comes first." — Source: §97.9(b) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** Question-set prep: each question set "must be prepared by a VE holding an Amateur Extra Class operator license" (Element 3 also by Advanced; Element 2 also by Advanced/General) using questions from the applicable pool. — Source: §97.507(a), (b) (verbatim in part).
- **FACT:** VE/operator classes: "The classes of amateur operator license grants are: Novice, Technician, General, Advanced, and Amateur Extra." — Source: §97.9(a) (verbatim).

## 11. External RF power amplifiers (§97.315, §97.317 — E1F03, E1F11)

- **FACT:** Amplifiers manufactured or imported for amateur use "must be certificated for use in the amateur service in accordance with subpart J of part 2," and "No amplifier capable of operation below 144 MHz may be constructed or modified by a non-amateur service licensee without a grant of certification from the FCC." — Source: §97.315(a) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Certification is not required if "The amplifier is constructed or modified by an amateur radio operator for use at an amateur station" (also exempt: used-condition dealer purchases and sales to an amateur operator). — Source: §97.315(b)(1), (b)(3) (verbatim in part; E1F03 answer D). [Watch: pool-era §97.315(b)(2), the pre-April 28, 1978 manufacture/marketing-waiver exemption, is now Reserved — 90 FR 57712, Dec 12, 2025; W3.]
- **FACT:** Certification standards: the amplifier must "Satisfy the spurious emission standards of § 97.307 (d) or (e) of this part, as applicable, when the amplifier is operated at the lesser of 1.5 kW PEP or its full output power and when the amplifier is placed in the 'standby' or 'off' positions while connected to the transmitter" (§97.317(a)(1), verbatim — E1F11 answer D); "Not be capable of amplifying the input RF power (driving signal) by more than 15 dB gain" (§97.317(a)(2)); "Exhibit no amplification (0 dB gain) between 26 MHz and 28 MHz" (§97.317(a)(3)).

## 12. Special Temporary Authority (§1.931 — E1F06)

- **FACT:** STA requests (Part 1): "In circumstances requiring immediate or temporary use of station in the Wireless Telecommunications Services, carriers may request special temporary authority (STA) to operate new or modified equipment," filed electronically "at least 10 days prior to the date of proposed operation." — Source: §1.931(a)(1) (verbatim in part; the pool cites [1.931] for E1F06, answer A "To provide for experimental amateur communications"). See W7 for the amateur-linkage nuance.

## Watch items — pool-era (2024-07-01) vs current (2026-07-28)

Diff of the two eCFR snapshots (full Part 97): the only substantive changes affecting amateur operation are W1–W5. Everything else in E1's cited sections is textually identical.

- **W1 — 60 m rewrite (91 FR 1430/1431, eff. Jan 14, 2026).** §97.301 tables now list "5.3515-5.3665" instead of "See §97.303(h)"; §97.303(h) restructured — old (h)(1) five-channel rule became new (h)(3) with FOUR discrete centers (5332, 5348, 5373, 5405 kHz; 5358.5 deleted) plus the contiguous 5351.5–5366.5 kHz segment; §97.305(c)(3)(iii) row now reads "5.332, 5.348, 5.3515-5.3665, 5.373, 5.405 MHz"; §97.307(f)(14) emission-designator table replaced by the plain 2.8 kHz bandwidth rule; §97.313(i) split into 100 W ERP (four channels) / 9.15 W ERP (segment). **Pool impact:** E1A06 (CW at channel center) and E1C01 (2.8 kHz) answers remain CORRECT under current text; E1A06's citation [97.303(h)(1)] now points at the fixed/mobile interference paragraph — the channel rules moved to (h)(3).
